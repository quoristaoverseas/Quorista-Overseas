01 · OVERVIEW

Taiwan at a glance

A technology-driven study destination with growing English-taught options, established universities and a distinctive mix of city, culture and nature travel.

Country context

Taiwan (Republic of China) is known for semiconductors, electronics, ICT, precision manufacturing and biotechnology. International students can choose public and private universities, universities of science and technology and specialist institutions. Mandarin remains the main language of daily life and many degree programmes; English-taught options exist at selected departments, particularly at graduate level. Always verify the language of the actual programme and its compulsory courses.

The official Study in Taiwan portal reports 140,417 international students in academic year 2025/26. This is a system-wide total, not a forecast of places available to new applicants.

CapitalTaipei
CurrencyNew Taiwan Dollar (TWD / NT$)

Time zoneUTC +8 · no daylight saving

Academic calendarAutumn usually September; spring often February, institution-dependent

Why students consider Taiwan

  • Strong academic and industrial ecosystem in semiconductors, electronics, ICT and engineering.
  • Undergraduate, master’s, doctoral and Mandarin-language study options.
  • Government and university scholarship routes with separate eligibility and deadlines.
  • Modern public transport and access to healthcare; required insurance and residence formalities still apply.

Why travellers visit

  • Night markets, temples, museums, food culture and contemporary city districts.
  • High-speed rail across the western corridor, plus metro and local rail networks.
  • Mountain, lake and coastal scenery, subject to weather and access notices.
  • October–April is often comfortable for city travel, though regional climate differs.

Use the official Study in Taiwan portal, universities and competent authorities to confirm current information.

English or Mandarin instruction?

Do not assume every course at an English-taught university or department is taught in English. Confirm compulsory courses, thesis supervision and test rules for the intended intake. Mandarin-taught programmes commonly require a suitable TOCFL level or other evidence accepted by the department.

For selected English-taught routes, Tamkang University lists IELTS 4.0, TOEFL iBT 57, TOEIC 550 or CEFR B1 as examples of accepted language evidence. This is not a Taiwan-wide minimum or a guarantee of eligibility.

Common application documents

  • Valid passport and online application.
  • Academic certificates and complete transcripts.
  • Accepted language test or evidence expressly permitted by the programme.
  • Study plan / statement of purpose; CV and references where requested.
  • Research proposal, portfolio, interview or supervisor correspondence where required.
  • Financial evidence, authenticated documents and translations where requested.

Scholarships and funding

The MOE Taiwan Scholarship can provide up to NT$40,000 per semester toward approved tuition and miscellaneous expenses, plus a monthly stipend of NT$15,000 for undergraduate or NT$20,000 for postgraduate recipients. Any amount above the cap and other living costs remain the student’s responsibility. Maximum award periods are generally four years for bachelor’s, two years for master’s and four years for doctoral study, subject to rules.

MOFA and Taiwan ICDF scholarships have separate nationality and diplomatic-relationship criteria. Do not assume an Indian applicant qualifies for every government scheme. Check the current India call with the Taipei Economic and Cultural Center and official scholarship portal. University awards have their own conditions and limited places.

Tuition and student living costs

A 2025–26 fee-schedule example for Tamkang University placed undergraduate tuition and miscellaneous fees at approximately NT$56,000–66,000 per semester and master’s fees at NT$58,000–67,000. This is one university’s historical planning example, not a national tariff or current fee quote.

Monthly living expenses may be around NT$18,000–25,000 as a broad planning estimate. Housing, deposits, insurance, transport, meals and personal spending can raise the total. Obtain the current fee schedule and housing details before committing.

Student visa and residence

Full-time degree students normally apply for the appropriate resident visa for study after receiving admission. BOCA requirements include an online visa form, passport, photographs, admission/registration evidence, academic and financial documents and a health certificate where applicable. The Taiwan mission may request more documents. After arrival, eligible students must complete the Alien Resident Certificate (ARC) process within the official deadline.

Mandarin language-centre students follow a different route. A visitor entry/TAC is not a substitute for a study visa.

BOCA: resident visas for foreign students ↗National Immigration Agency ↗

Part-time work and after graduation

International degree students generally need a Ministry of Labor work permit before employment; the usual term-time limit is 20 hours per week, subject to current rules. Foreign students in Mandarin language programmes may apply only after studying for at least one year. Do not budget on assumed employment income.

From 2026, eligible graduates of associate degree or higher programmes in Taiwan may apply for a post-graduation residence extension for up to two years (one year, renewable once). During the approved extension, eligible graduates can work without a separate work permit. This is a temporary transition, not permanent residence.

04 · INTERNATIONAL TOURISM

Explore Taiwan

Pair a few well-connected destinations with enough time to enjoy food, museums, temples and local neighbourhoods. Travel times and access change with weather and maintenance.

Where to go

  • Taipei — 3–4 nights: Taipei 101, National Palace Museum, Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall, Longshan Temple, Beitou hot springs and Shilin/Raohe/Ningxia night markets.
  • Kaohsiung — 2 nights: Lotus Pond, Pier-2 Art Center, Cijin Island and night markets.
  • Taichung — 1–2 nights: city culture, museums, markets and central Taiwan day trips.
  • Tainan — 1–2 nights: historic temples, old streets, heritage sites and traditional food.
  • Hualien / Taroko: add only after checking park notices, road controls, trail status and weather. Post-earthquake and typhoon conditions can change quickly.

Best time to visit

October to April is often comfortable for northern Taiwan city travel. Taipei is cooler in winter; Kaohsiung is generally warmer. May to November overlaps with typhoon season, and heavy rain, landslides, rail disruption or short-notice closures can affect outdoor plans. Spring conditions and blossom timing vary by elevation and year.

06 · TRANSPORT & COSTS

Getting around and planning your budget

Local and intercity transport

  • Taipei MRT: single journeys typically NT$20–65; compare stored-value cards and passes against your route.
  • City buses: commonly NT$15–30 per ride; check local fare rules.
  • Airport MRT: Taoyuan Airport to Taipei Main Station takes about 35–40 minutes on express services.
  • High-speed rail: Taipei to Zuoying (Kaohsiung) takes about 1 hour 35 minutes on fastest services; standard full fare is around NT$1,490 one way. Schedules and discounts vary.
  • Taxi / ride-hailing: useful for late arrivals or luggage; use licensed services and check fare method.

07 · FOOD & EXPERIENCES

What to see, do and eat

Signature attractions

  • Taipei 101 Observatory: skyline views; standard tickets have been listed around NT$600. Check current hours and last entry.
  • National Palace Museum: major art and history collections; standard adult admission has been listed around NT$350. Check closure days and exhibition access.
  • Lotus Pond and Pier-2 Art Center: Kaohsiung landmarks; outdoor areas may be free while exhibitions charge.
  • Jiufen and Shifen: popular day trip from Taipei; tour inclusions and transport vary.
Taipei 101 ↗National Palace Museum ↗Kaohsiung Travel ↗

Food and dietary needs

Try beef noodle soup (niu rou mian), xiaolongbao, lu rou fan, oyster omelette, bubble milk tea and mango shaved ice. Street-food and casual-dining prices vary; NT$60–280 per item is a rough planning range, not a fixed menu price.

Taiwan has a well-established vegetarian food culture. Look for 素食 / su shi labels, but clarify meat stock, fish sauce, egg, dairy, garlic and onion. Halal-certified and Muslim-friendly dining options are available in larger cities; verify current certification and location before relying on a restaurant.

08 · INDIAN TRAVELLER ENTRY GUIDE

Understand Taiwan visitor entry

Rules depend on passport, purpose and supporting documents. Confirm eligibility directly with Taiwan’s authorities before buying non-refundable travel.

Indian ordinary passport holders need prior entry authorisation. Some Indian nationals may qualify for Taiwan’s online Travel Authorization Certificate (TAC). Official conditions include a regular passport valid for at least six months on arrival, a confirmed onward/return air or boat ticket, no previous blue-collar employment in Taiwan, and a qualifying current or recently expired (within 10 years) visa/residence document issued by Australia, Canada, Japan, Korea, New Zealand, a Schengen country, the UK or the US—or a qualifying previous Taiwan visa/resident card. A qualifying document alone does not grant entry: apply for and obtain the TAC before travel.

Travel Authorization Certificate

  • Official online TAC application is currently free.
  • Once approved, the certificate is valid for 90 days and allows multiple entries during that period.
  • Each stay is up to 30 days, counted from the day after arrival, under the published rule.
  • Print the approved certificate and carry the qualifying visa/residence evidence and onward ticket for inspection.
  • Approval and entry remain subject to immigration inspection; do not rely on assumed instant approval.

Visitor visa and special routes

Travellers who do not qualify for TAC should contact the Taipei Economic and Cultural Center serving their place of residence for the correct visitor-visa category, documents, appointment and current fees. Certain designated group-tour and business routes have separate eligibility and must not be presented as a general individual-tourist eVisa.

Prepare passport, itinerary, accommodation, onward travel, financial evidence and employment/business documents as required. The mission may request more information and retains the decision.

09 · SAFETY & PRACTICAL INFORMATION

Before you travel

Weather, earthquakes and outdoor access

Taiwan experiences typhoons, heavy rain and earthquakes. Follow Central Weather Administration alerts, transport notices and local instructions. Mountain trails, coastal roads and Taroko-area routes may close or reopen at short notice after severe weather or damage. Do not assume a destination shown in an itinerary is currently accessible.

Health and insurance

Arrange travel insurance covering medical treatment, disruption and evacuation appropriate to your activities. Check prescription-medicine import rules before departure and carry medicines in original packaging with supporting prescriptions where appropriate.

Money, connectivity and emergencies

  • Currency: New Taiwan Dollar (NT$). Carry some cash for smaller shops and night markets; card acceptance varies.
  • EasyCard can be useful on metro, buses and selected convenience-store purchases; acceptance varies by service.
  • Tourist SIM/eSIM packages are available from major providers; compare data, duration and device compatibility.
  • Police: 110 · Fire and ambulance: 119 · Taiwan foreigner/tourist helpline: 1990; confirm language support and availability.

Travel advisories and local conditions change. Check your own government’s current travel advice and Taiwan’s official weather, transport and park notices immediately before departure.
