01 · COUNTRY OVERVIEW

China at a glance

China is one of the world's largest countries, with major economic, academic and cultural centres spread across distinct regions. The right city can shape a student's academic experience or a visitor's itinerary just as much as the country itself.

A destination with many dimensions

From Beijing's imperial heritage and national institutions to Shanghai's international business environment, Chengdu's food culture and the landscapes of Guilin and Yunnan, China offers varied experiences. Mandarin Chinese is the national standard language; local languages and regional varieties are also widely used. International visitors should plan for language and digital-payment differences before arrival.

For students, China offers a large higher-education sector, research-intensive universities, specialist institutions and programmes in both Chinese and English. Programme language, recognition, professional licensing and the suitability of a course for an individual career plan should be checked carefully.

02 · STUDY ABROAD

Build your academic pathway in China

China's universities span comprehensive research institutions, specialist science and technology universities, medical universities, language programmes and international joint institutes. The best option depends on subject, language of instruction, budget, academic profile and future plans.

1. Why study in China?

  • Wide choice of institutions and disciplines, including engineering, computer science, business, medicine, economics, sciences and humanities.
  • Research and innovation ecosystems in major cities, with links to technology, manufacturing, finance and healthcare sectors.
  • English-taught options at selected universities alongside Chinese-medium degree routes.
  • Potential access to university, provincial and Chinese Government Scholarship opportunities.
  • Opportunity to develop Mandarin language skills and cross-cultural experience.
A university's reputation alone is not enough. Confirm the exact programme, language, curriculum, clinical or practical training arrangements, degree recognition and eligibility for your intended career.

2. Education system

International applicants can generally consider undergraduate, master's and doctoral degrees, as well as non-degree Chinese-language or visiting-scholar programmes. Typical study durations vary by discipline and institution.

PathwayTypical durationUsual prior qualification
Bachelor'sAbout 4–5 years; some professional courses differSenior secondary / Class 12 or equivalent
Master'sAbout 2–3 yearsRecognised bachelor's degree
DoctoralOften 3–4 years; programme-specificRelevant master's degree or equivalent
Chinese language / non-degreeVaries by courseSet by the host institution

These are planning guides, not a promise of duration. Research, clinical, professional and integrated programmes may have different structures.

05 · PROGRAMMES & TUITION

Programmes and tuition fees

China offers programmes across a broad range of disciplines. Tuition is set by each institution and can vary significantly between undergraduate, postgraduate, clinical, laboratory-based and specialist courses.

Popular subject areas

  • Engineering, robotics, automation and advanced manufacturing
  • Computer science, artificial intelligence and data-related fields
  • Business, finance, economics and international trade
  • Medicine, public health, pharmacy and life sciences
  • Architecture, design, environmental science and agriculture
  • Chinese language, culture, history and international relations

Indicative tuition reference

Published examples show substantial variation. For instance, Tsinghua University's current undergraduate fee schedule lists many Chinese-English programmes around CNY 26,000–40,000 per year, with some specialist English-taught programmes priced differently. Beijing's municipal education portal also publishes institution-level tuition examples, but some entries are older.

Planning approach: treat these as institution-specific references only. Always request the current fee schedule for the exact programme and intake, including application, registration, laboratory, insurance and other compulsory charges.

06 · SCHOLARSHIPS

Scholarship opportunities

Funding may be available through national, provincial, municipal and university schemes. Awards can be full or partial and may cover different combinations of tuition, accommodation, insurance and living support.

Chinese Government Scholarship

CSC-administered awards may support eligible undergraduate, master's, doctoral and other study categories. Routes and eligibility depend on the scholarship type and annual call.

Provincial & municipal awards

Some local governments and education authorities offer scholarships for international students enrolled at eligible institutions in their region.

University scholarships

Universities may offer merit-based or programme-specific awards, tuition reductions or other support. Criteria, renewal conditions and deadlines differ.

Scholarship planning: confirm whether the award is open to Indian applicants, whether a separate university admission is required, which costs are covered, renewal conditions and whether international airfare is excluded. Never assume that a scholarship is guaranteed with admission.

07 · ADMISSION REQUIREMENTS

What may be required?

Universities assess academic suitability, language readiness and supporting documents. Requirements differ by programme, level, university and intake.

Academic and language criteria

  • Class 12 or equivalent for undergraduate admission.
  • Relevant bachelor's degree for master's admission.
  • Relevant master's degree for doctoral admission.
  • HSK scores for Chinese-medium programmes where required.
  • IELTS, TOEFL or accepted alternatives for English-medium programmes where required; some institutions consider prior medium of instruction or conduct an assessment.
  • Portfolio, entrance assessment, interview, research proposal or subject prerequisites for selected programmes.

Common document checklist

  • Valid passport and completed application form.
  • Academic certificates, transcripts and grading details.
  • Language test results, if required.
  • Personal statement or study/research plan.
  • Recommendation letters for selected postgraduate courses.
  • CV, portfolio or work experience evidence where applicable.
  • Medical examination, financial evidence and other documents requested by the university.
  • Certified translations or notarised copies where specified.

09 · STUDENT VISA GUIDANCE

China student visa: X1 and X2

Student visa category depends primarily on the intended duration of study. The Chinese authorities make the final decision on visa issuance and conditions.

X1 visa

For study programmes lasting more than 180 days. The official guidance lists the admission letter and the Confirmation Form for Study in China (JW201 or JW202) among the required supporting documents. After entry, X1 holders must apply for a residence permit within 30 days.

X2 visa

For study programmes lasting no more than 180 days. An admission letter from the receiving institution is required; other supporting documents may be requested according to the application and consular review.

For applicants in India, the Chinese Embassy's notice dated 22 October 2026 describes online application and document review, followed by passport/document submission at the relevant embassy or consulate. It states short-term visa applicants staying no more than 180 days are exempt from fingerprint collection until 31 December 2026, subject to the notice's conditions. Long-term X1 applicants are generally required to attend for fingerprint collection. Recheck the latest notice before applying.

10 · ACCOMMODATION & LIVING

Accommodation and living expenses

Living costs depend on city, campus location, room type and lifestyle. Beijing and Shanghai may require a higher budget than many smaller university cities.

Accommodation options

  • University dormitories: convenient for campus access; availability, room type, fees and booking windows vary.
  • Private rental: offers more independence but requires careful review of lease terms, deposits, location and local registration requirements.
  • Shared apartments: may reduce costs but students should check commute times, utilities and house rules.
  • Temporary arrival stay: arrange a suitable short stay if permanent housing is not confirmed before travel.

Indicative student budget

As a broad planning reference, some university-published examples place everyday living costs in the region of approximately CNY 3,000–6,000 per month in selected Shanghai student settings, excluding tuition and depending on accommodation and lifestyle. Beijing can be higher: Peking University's published guidance cites accommodation around CNY 4,000 per month, meals around CNY 1,500 and transport/miscellaneous costs separately.

Use these only as examples, not a universal China-wide budget. Confirm dormitory rates, meals, insurance, utilities, local transport and personal spending with the chosen university.

03 · TOURISM & TRAVEL

Experience China beyond the guidebook

China's regions offer very different travel styles: imperial history, contemporary city life, mountain scenery, river landscapes, cuisine, shopping and cultural experiences. Itineraries should account for travel distances and local booking arrangements.

11. Why visit China?

China appeals to travellers interested in heritage, architecture, food, nature, shopping, technology and contemporary urban culture. Visitors can combine UNESCO-listed landmarks with modern skylines, traditional gardens, scenic river towns and regional food experiences. A well-paced trip usually focuses on one or two regions rather than trying to cover the entire country in a single visit.

13 · BEST TIME TO VISIT

Choose your season

China's climate varies significantly from north to south and from coast to inland. Spring and autumn are often comfortable for multi-city sightseeing, but the best dates depend on the region.

SeasonTypical conditionsTravel considerations
Spring · March–MayMilder weather in many regions; flowers and greener landscapes.Good for city sightseeing and gardens; check regional rainfall and temperature.
Summer · June–AugustHot and humid in many eastern and southern cities; rainy periods in some regions.Mountain and higher-altitude areas may be attractive; plan for heat, rain and peak domestic travel.
Autumn · September–NovemberOften pleasant in northern and central destinations, with autumn colour.Popular sightseeing period; book transport and hotels early around holidays.
Winter · December–FebruaryCold in northern China; milder in parts of the south.Suitable for winter scenery and selected festivals; pack for regional conditions.
Avoid assuming that one season suits the entire country. Chinese public holidays and the Spring Festival travel period can create high demand and crowding. Check local calendars before fixing dates.
14 · TOURIST VISA INFORMATION

Entry rules for Indian passport holders

Visa requirements must be checked for the traveller's passport, purpose, duration and itinerary before tickets are purchased.

Indian ordinary passport holders should not assume that China is visa-free. The Chinese Embassy in India publishes the relevant visa application procedure and supporting-document requirements. The general visa-free transit or unilateral visa-waiver policies announced for certain nationalities do not automatically apply to Indian citizens. Visa-free entry rules may also differ for mainland China, Hong Kong and Macao.

For a tourism visit, applicants generally need the relevant visa category and supporting itinerary/accommodation or invitation information as required by the current consular instructions. The final visa decision, validity, number of entries and permitted stay are determined by the Chinese authorities.

15 · FLIGHTS FROM INDIA

Getting to China from India

Air connectivity and operating schedules can change. Direct and connecting options depend on the departure city, destination airport, airline approvals and travel date.

Common gateways to compare

  • Delhi–Shanghai: Air India announced the resumption of non-stop services from 1 February 2026, with a published schedule of four weekly flights at launch.
  • Delhi–Guangzhou: airlines have announced non-stop services; check current operating dates and availability with the airline.
  • Kolkata–Guangzhou: direct connectivity has also been announced by IndiGo, subject to the airline's live schedule.
  • Other destinations: many itineraries may require a connection through another Chinese or international hub.

Before booking

  • Compare total journey time, baggage allowance and connection protection.
  • Check arrival airport and onward domestic transfer requirements.
  • Allow sufficient time between separate tickets.
  • Confirm visa validity and permitted entry dates before departure.
  • Recheck schedules directly with airlines; published route announcements are not a live fare or seat guarantee.

16 · HOTELS & ACCOMMODATION

Where to stay

Accommodation options range from international hotel chains and premium properties to business hotels, boutique stays, serviced apartments and hostels.

Hotels

Useful for short visits, multi-city itineraries and travellers who prefer front-desk support. Compare location, cancellation policy, breakfast, room occupancy and accepted payment methods.

Serviced apartments

Can suit longer stays or families needing more space. Confirm registration rules, services, deposits and whether the property accepts foreign guests.

Location strategy

Choose accommodation near the attractions or transport interchange relevant to your itinerary. A cheaper room far from the city centre may increase daily travel time and costs.

17 · LOCAL TRANSPORTATION

Moving around China

Major cities have extensive public transport networks, while intercity high-speed rail connects many major urban centres.

City transport

  • Metro: efficient in major cities; station signage and ticket machines may include English in central areas.
  • City buses: economical, but route information may be less straightforward for visitors unfamiliar with Chinese.
  • Taxis and ride-hailing: useful for door-to-door journeys; confirm app access, payment setup and destination details.
  • Walking and cycling: practical in selected districts and scenic areas; observe local traffic rules.

Intercity travel

  • High-speed rail: convenient between many major cities; book using passport details and allow station security time.
  • Domestic flights: useful for long distances or remote regions.
  • Regional trains and coaches: may serve smaller destinations and scenic routes.
  • Travel apps: prepare access to maps, translation, ticketing and payment tools before arrival.
