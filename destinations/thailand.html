01 · DESTINATION OVERVIEW

Thailand at a glance

A Southeast Asian destination with established public universities, a growing range of English-taught degrees, varied student cities and a strong tourism network. Programme language, entry requirements and costs must always be checked at faculty level.

Education and lifestyle

Thailand offers undergraduate, master’s and doctoral routes across public research universities, autonomous institutions, private universities and specialist colleges. International programmes are concentrated in selected faculties rather than offered across every subject.

Bangkok is the largest education and employment hub. Chiang Mai offers a more relaxed northern-city environment, while Khon Kaen and the Bangkok metropolitan region provide additional university options. Thai is the main language in everyday life and in many domestic degree programmes.

02 · STUDY ABROAD

Academic pathways in Thailand

Choose a course based on academic fit, language of instruction, recognition, total cost and the university’s support for international students.

Undergraduate · Bachelor’s

Most bachelor’s degrees take about four years. Medicine, dentistry, veterinary science, pharmacy and architecture may take longer.

  • Completed upper-secondary education equivalent to Grade 12 / Year 13.
  • Foreign qualifications such as A-Levels, IB or GED may require formal equivalency review.
  • Some programmes require SAT, ACT, subject tests, portfolio assessment or interview.
  • Academic thresholds differ by faculty; competitive programmes may expect stronger grades.

Postgraduate · Master’s and PhD

Master’s degrees commonly take one to two years; doctoral study often takes three to five years or longer, depending on research and programme structure.

  • Master’s: relevant recognised bachelor’s degree; some courses require work experience or a portfolio.
  • PhD: relevant prior degree, research proposal, references and supervisor or faculty fit.
  • GPA requirements vary; many programmes use a minimum around 2.50–2.75/4.00, but this is not universal.
  • Interviews, entrance tests and faculty-specific prerequisites may apply.
LANGUAGE OF INSTRUCTION

English-taught or Thai-taught?

Thailand’s domestic university system primarily teaches in Thai. English-language options are offered through selected international programmes and faculties.

English-taught programmes

Some programmes use IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT, CU-TEP or other accepted tests. A broad planning range may be IELTS 6.0–6.5 or TOEFL iBT 79–90+, but each university and faculty sets its own accepted tests, minimum bands and validity period. PTE Academic or Duolingo may be accepted by some programmes and not others.

Do not assume a medium-of-instruction letter replaces a test. Obtain written confirmation from the programme before applying.

Thai-taught programmes

Thai proficiency is generally required where the degree is delivered in Thai. International programmes may not require Thai for admission, but basic Thai can be valuable for daily life, placements and integration.

Some universities offer additional language support or preparatory courses. Availability and extra fees must be confirmed with the institution.

Scholarships and funding

Chulalongkorn — Chula LINK: the 2026 international graduate call covered tuition, a monthly living allowance and round-trip airfare for eligible non-Thai master’s and doctoral students who had secured admission. The published second-semester 2026 deadline was 31 October 2026 and is now closed; future calls and coverage must be checked afresh.

Mahidol University International College: its 2026 scholarship information includes a 50% tuition waiver route for eligible Asian students from UN-designated low-income countries, subject to academic and application conditions. Indian eligibility must be checked against the current country list and call.

Other university and government awards may be available by faculty, nationality or nomination route. Never assume that a scholarship is automatic or that it covers all living expenses.

Tuition and living-cost planning

At Chulalongkorn, published standard international-student tuition ranges by faculty group from about THB 76,000–106,000 per semester at undergraduate level and THB 76,000–133,900 at graduate level. Special programmes can cost substantially more and may add programme fees.

For Bangkok, a practical student living-cost planning range is approximately THB 18,000–40,000 per month, depending mainly on rent, food, transport and personal spending. Chiang Mai may be less expensive. Confirm fees for the exact programme and intake.

ADMISSION REQUIREMENTS

Prepare a complete application

Universities set their own document, equivalency, language and selection requirements. The following is a general checklist, not a guarantee of eligibility.

Common documents

  • Passport biodata page and recent passport-size photograph.
  • Academic certificates, transcripts and grading scale; certified English translations where needed.
  • Accepted English or Thai language evidence, if required.
  • Statement of purpose or study plan.
  • CV, recommendation letters and employment evidence for selected graduate programmes.
  • Portfolio for design, architecture or arts; research proposal for doctoral applications.
  • Financial or sponsor evidence where requested by the university or visa process.

Typical application sequence

02 · SELECT

04 · ENROL

Review the offer, fees, deposits and university visa instructions.

Student visa route: The Non-Immigrant ED Plus category is intended for eligible full-time degree students at bachelor’s level or above. Other study purposes—including language courses, short courses and some exchange or training routes—may require a different ED category. The university and the Royal Thai Embassy must confirm the correct category before travel. ED Plus provides a possible one-year post-graduation extension for eligible graduates to seek employment or undertake other activities; it is not itself a work permit. Employment requires the appropriate immigration status and work authorisation.

04 · INTERNATIONAL TOURISM

Explore Thailand

From Bangkok’s temples and riverfront to northern cultural centres and southern beaches, Thailand offers varied travel styles. Build a route around season, travel time and the kind of holiday you want.

Best time to visit

Bangkok and much of central Thailand: November to February is generally cooler and drier. March and April are hotter; the southwest monsoon brings more rain from around May to October, with September often particularly wet.

Phuket and the Andaman coast: November to April is generally the more favourable beach and island season. May to October can bring rougher seas, heavy rain and cancelled boat excursions.

Chiang Mai and the north: November to February is usually more comfortable. The hot season and seasonal smoke or haze can affect air quality, especially around late winter and spring. Check local conditions close to departure.

Flights from India

Bangkok is the main international gateway, with flights from major Indian cities depending on airline schedules and season. Non-stop services may be available from Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru. Phuket can be reached on selected direct services or via Bangkok.

Airfares from India vary by departure city, season, airline and booking date. Compare live fares for Bangkok and Phuket, including baggage, airport and connection conditions, before booking.

05 · DESTINATIONS

Places to include in your itinerary

Bangkok

Grand Palace, Wat Pho, Wat Arun, Jim Thompson House, river ferries, markets and shopping districts. Allow around three nights for a first visit.

Phuket

Patong, Kata and Karon beaches, Old Phuket Town, island excursions to Phi Phi and Phang Nga Bay. Allow around three nights or more.

Chiang Mai

Old City temples, mountain scenery, craft markets, northern Thai food and carefully selected ethical nature experiences.

Krabi

Railay, limestone cliffs, beaches and boat trips. Sea conditions and operator safety matter during the monsoon period.

Ayutthaya

Historic temple ruins and UNESCO-listed heritage landscapes, often visited as a day trip from Bangkok.

Pattaya

Beachfront hotels, family attractions and entertainment, reachable from Bangkok by road.

07 · LOCAL TRANSPORT

Getting around

Bangkok and city travel

Bangkok has BTS Skytrain, MRT, buses, the Airport Rail Link, river boats, metered taxis and ride-hailing services. Use official station information for current fares and operating hours. In taxis, request the meter; agree tuk-tuk fares before departure.

Indicative city transport costs from the research dossier include BTS trips around THB 17–62, MRT around THB 17–45 and the Chao Phraya Express Boat around THB 16, subject to route and fare revisions.

Phuket and intercity travel

Phuket has local buses and the Smart Bus on selected routes, as well as taxis and ride-hailing. Airport-to-hotel transfers can be a significant cost, especially for distant beach areas.

Domestic flights connect Bangkok with Phuket and Chiang Mai. Trains and intercity coaches are alternatives for selected routes; compare journey duration, departure station and luggage rules before booking.

Food and dining

Popular dishes include Pad Thai, Tom Yum Goong, green curry, Som Tam, boat noodles, Khao Man Gai and mango sticky rice. Street-food meals and food courts can be inexpensive, while hotel dining and tourist-area restaurants cost more.

  • Street-food dish: about THB 50–150.
  • Simple noodle or rice meal: about THB 60–100.
  • Mid-range Thai meal: about THB 500–900 for a three-course meal with drinks.

Dietary options

Halal food is widely available in parts of Bangkok and Phuket; look for recognised halal certification where needed. Vegetarian and vegan choices are available, including restaurants serving “Jay” food. Indian restaurants are present in Bangkok and popular tourist districts such as Patong.

Clearly explain dietary restrictions when ordering, as fish sauce, shrimp paste and meat-based stock may be used in dishes that otherwise appear vegetable-based.

09 · TOURIST ENTRY

Thailand entry guidance for Indian travellers

Indian ordinary passport holders: the Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi confirms a 30-day visa exemption for tourism only, effective from 15 September 2026 until further announcement. Do not rely on older 60-day information. This exemption does not authorise study or employment. Travellers seeking a longer stay should check the appropriate e-Visa category and current conditions with the Embassy.

Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC): all non-Thai nationals entering by air, land or sea must submit the free online arrival card within three days before arrival (including the arrival date). Use only the official immigration portal. TDAC is an arrival declaration, not a visa.

Carry a passport with adequate validity, onward/return travel details, accommodation information and evidence of funds if requested. Final admission is decided by Thai immigration officers.

11 · TRAVEL SAFETY & PRACTICAL INFORMATION

Check advisories before you travel

Travel advice can change quickly and different governments may use different warning boundaries. Check the advisory issued by your own government and your insurer before confirming travel.

Regional security warning: current advisories from Australia and the United Kingdom warn against travel to areas near the Thailand–Cambodia land border because of armed clashes and landmine risks. Some advisories also recommend avoiding or reconsidering travel to parts of the far south, including Yala, Pattani and Narathiwat. Do not include affected border or southern areas in a standard leisure itinerary without a fresh risk review.

Local laws and personal safety

  • Vapes and e-cigarettes are prohibited; do not bring them into Thailand.
  • Drug offences carry severe penalties. Do not carry controlled substances across borders.
  • Defamation or insult laws concerning the monarchy are strict, including online conduct.
  • Use licensed transport, wear a helmet on motorcycles and be cautious with jet-ski rentals and informal tour operators.
  • Check air quality in Bangkok and Chiang Mai, particularly during seasonal haze.

Useful emergency contacts

  • Police: 191
  • Tourist Police (English support): 1155
  • Medical emergency / ambulance: 1669
  • Fire: 199
  • Immigration Bureau: 1178
