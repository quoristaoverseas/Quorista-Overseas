01 · COUNTRY OVERVIEW

United Arab Emirates at a glance

United Arab Emirates offers distinct education, career and travel possibilities. The best city and pathway depend on the subject, language, budget, season and personal circumstances.

Understanding the destination

The UAE is a regional higher-education hub with federal universities, private institutions and international branch campuses. English-taught degrees are common, particularly in Dubai and Abu Dhabi. Check campus accreditation, programme accreditation and whether the qualification meets requirements in the country where you intend to work.

Dubai’s skyline and desert experiences, Abu Dhabi’s cultural district and mosques, Sharjah’s museums, Ras Al Khaimah’s mountains and Fujairah’s coastline make the Emirates suitable for city breaks and family travel.

02 · STUDY ABROAD

Build an academic pathway in the United Arab Emirates

The UAE offers English-taught degrees, international branch campuses and established public and private universities. Choose by course quality, recognition, total cost and realistic admission fit—not by city or university name alone.

Why consider the UAE?

  • English-medium undergraduate and postgraduate options across business, computing, engineering, health sciences, architecture and other fields.
  • International campuses and locally established universities in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah and other emirates.
  • Merit awards and tuition discounts at selected institutions, subject to nationality, grades, course and intake.
  • Multicultural campuses and access to regional employers, subject to immigration and work-permit rules.

Academic pathways

  • Foundation / pathway: selected institutions offer preparatory or bridging routes where school subjects, grades or English scores do not meet direct entry.
  • Bachelor’s: usually 3–4 years; engineering and some regulated disciplines may take longer.
  • Master’s / MBA: commonly 1–2 years; relevant prior study, work experience or specific prerequisites may apply.
  • Doctoral: research fit, proposal, supervisor availability and prior academic performance are central.

Duration, programme availability and entry rules vary by institution and discipline.

03 · STUDENT CITIES

Compare the main study locations

Housing, campus location, commute, programme availability and personal budget can matter more than the emirate’s reputation.

Dubai

The broadest concentration of international branch campuses and private higher education providers, with extensive business, technology, media and hospitality networks. Accommodation and daily living costs can be high, especially in central and waterfront districts.

Abu Dhabi

A strong option to research for engineering, science, AI, business and research-led study, including Khalifa University and NYU Abu Dhabi. Compare campus location and transport before selecting housing.

Sharjah

Home to the University of Sharjah, American University of Sharjah and Al Qasimia University. Compare subject fit, scholarship conditions and the commute if you plan to live or work in Dubai.

Al Ain

Home to United Arab Emirates University, with a different pace and cost profile from Dubai. A practical location to compare for selected sciences, engineering, education and health-related disciplines.

English language & admission

IELTS Academic, TOEFL or an institution’s own placement test may be accepted. As examples only, University of Dubai publishes IELTS Academic 5.0 as a general undergraduate benchmark and course-specific requirements; Abu Dhabi University generally lists IELTS 6.0 or an approved equivalent for many postgraduate programmes. Some universities consider prior English-medium study or an MOI letter in defined cases, but this is not a universal waiver.

  • Grade 12 or equivalent, with subject and grade prerequisites for bachelor’s entry.
  • A recognised bachelor’s degree for master’s study; relevant discipline and CGPA requirements apply.
  • Attestation and UAE Ministry of Education equivalency may be required for foreign certificates.
  • PhD applicants may need a research proposal, references, CV and supervisor alignment.

Tuition & living-cost planning

One research benchmark for the University of Dubai is approximately AED 52,000–65,000 per year for bachelor’s study and AED 78,000–100,000 for selected master’s programmes. These are institution-specific planning figures, not UAE-wide tuition rates.

A broad student living-cost estimate is around AED 3,000–8,000+ per month, with housing as the largest variable. Shared accommodation, residence halls and private studios differ substantially by emirate and neighbourhood.

Budget separately for deposits, registration, visa/residence formalities, Emirates ID, insurance, books, transport and annual fee changes. Ask the institution for a current itemised schedule before making a payment.

06 · ADMISSIONS & STUDENT RESIDENCE

From application to arrival

Admission and immigration are separate decisions. Confirm the sponsor, required documents, fees, insurance and residence process with the institution and relevant authority before paying deposits or booking travel.

STEP 01

Profile assessment

Review academic history, subject prerequisites, English evidence, budget and intended intake.

STEP 02

Course shortlist

Compare official course pages, campus, accreditation, tuition, scholarships and realistic entry requirements.

STEP 03

Application

Prepare attested certificates and transcripts, language evidence and any course-specific documents; complete tests or interviews if required.

STEP 04

Offer & enrolment

Review the offer, refund rules, deposit and fee schedule before accepting and completing enrolment.

STEP 05

Student residence

Follow the accredited institution’s or eligible sponsor’s instructions for the residence permit, medical screening, Emirates ID and insurance.

Common document checklist

  • Passport copy and recent photographs.
  • Grade 10–12 or bachelor’s certificates and transcripts, as relevant.
  • Attestation and equivalency documents where required.
  • IELTS / TOEFL / accepted alternative or written language waiver, if approved.
  • CV, statement of purpose, references, portfolio or research proposal where applicable.
  • Financial, sponsor and health-insurance evidence requested by the institution or immigration authority.

Student residence, work & after study

Eligible international students may be sponsored by an accredited university or, in qualifying cases, a UAE-resident parent. The residence route is separate from a tourist visa; validity and renewal depend on the programme, sponsor and competent authority.

Student employment or training requires the relevant permit and conditions. Do not rely on a blanket “20 hours per week” rule or assume a student residence visa alone authorises work.

ICP guidance provides a 180-day post-study grace period for students after completing studies. A separate 60-, 90- or 120-day jobseeker visit visa exists only for applicants who meet its degree, skill/university and financial criteria; it is not a work permit.

07 · INTERNATIONAL TOURISM

Explore the United Arab Emirates

A multi-city visit can combine Dubai’s modern landmarks, Abu Dhabi’s cultural attractions and a desert experience. Plan around the season, current travel advisories, flight operations and the actual time needed between emirates.

Important travel-safety check: Regional security conditions remain changeable. As at 20 November 2026, the UK FCDO warns of a deeply unpredictable regional situation and a high risk of sudden escalation; Australia advises a high degree of caution; and the US advisory is Level 3, “Reconsider travel”. These advisories are not identical. Review your own government’s latest advice and airline/airport notices immediately before booking and departure. Quorista should not treat an itinerary or package as cleared for travel solely because flights are displayed for sale.

Best time to visit

November to April is generally more comfortable for outdoor sightseeing, beaches and desert excursions. Summer, especially June to September, brings intense heat and warm nights; schedule indoor attractions, early-morning activities and rest breaks. Ramadan, Eid and major events can change opening hours, traffic and hotel prices.

Flights from India

Major Indian gateways connect with Dubai (DXB), Abu Dhabi (AUH) and Sharjah (SHJ), with non-stop and connecting options depending on airline and date. Flight times are route-dependent, usually around 3–4 hours from major north and west Indian gateways. Fares and schedules change frequently; compare baggage, airport, transit requirements and cancellation terms directly with the airline.

08 · CITIES & EXPERIENCES

Where to go and what to see

Choose two or three areas rather than trying to cover every emirate in a short trip.

Dubai

Burj Khalifa and Downtown, Dubai Frame, Museum of the Future, Dubai Mall and Aquarium, Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, Dubai Creek and licensed desert safaris. Allow at least three to four nights for a first visit.

Abu Dhabi

Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, Louvre Abu Dhabi, Qasr Al Watan, Corniche and Saadiyat Cultural District. Check mosque registration and dress requirements and book timed attractions in advance.

Sharjah

Explore the museums, Heart of Sharjah, heritage areas and Al Noor Island. A practical day trip from Dubai, subject to traffic and event schedules.

Ras Al Khaimah

Mountain scenery around Jebel Jais, beaches and outdoor activities. Check weather, road access, operator credentials and activity safety requirements before travelling.

Fujairah

East-coast beaches, mountain scenery, heritage sites and marine excursions. Check sea conditions and licensed diving or boat operators.

Desert experiences

Licensed afternoon and evening safaris may include dune drives, camel experiences, sandboarding and dinner entertainment. Check inclusions, pickup area, safety rules and child suitability before paying.

FOOD & DINING

Try Emirati flavours and international dining

The UAE’s food scene ranges from traditional Emirati dishes and casual cafés to regional and international restaurants.

What to try

Look for Machboos (spiced rice with meat or seafood), Luqaimat (sweet dumplings), shawarma and Arabic mezze. Heritage restaurants and local cafés can be a good introduction to Emirati and wider Gulf cuisine.

Halal food is widely available. Vegetarian and vegan choices are common in Indian, Lebanese and international restaurants; confirm ingredients and preparation directly with the restaurant.

Indicative meal costs

  • Casual / street-food meal: AED 10–35.
  • Mid-range restaurant: AED 60–150 per person.
  • Premium dining: AED 250–600+ per person.

Prices vary by city, venue, service charges and drinks. These are planning estimates, not menu quotations.

10 · LOCAL TRANSPORT

Getting around the Emirates

Dubai has the most extensive urban rail network. Abu Dhabi and the other emirates rely more on buses, taxis and road transfers; check the best first- and last-mile connection for your hotel.

Within cities

  • Dubai: Metro, tram and buses use the Nol ticketing system; fares depend on zones and ticket type. Airport-to-city taxis and ride-hailing are widely available.
  • Abu Dhabi: Public buses use the Hafilat card; taxis and ride-hailing fill gaps between attractions.
  • Sharjah: Local buses and taxis operate, but cross-emirate commutes can take longer during peak traffic.
  • Car hire: Check licence, insurance, deposit, tolls, parking and cross-emirate terms before collecting a vehicle.

Between Dubai and Abu Dhabi

Intercity buses remain a value option; check current E100/E101 routes, departure stations and timetables. Rail station access and journey times vary, so include the transfer to and from the station when comparing it with a bus or taxi.

11 · VISITOR VISA & ARRIVAL

Check the correct entry route before booking

Visa requirements depend on passport nationality and, for some Indian travellers, qualifying visas or residence permits held in another country.

Indian ordinary passport holders

Most Indian visitors need a pre-arranged UAE tourist or visit visa. A limited visa-on-arrival facility may apply to eligible Indian nationals holding a qualifying foreign visa, Green Card or residence permit and meeting the validity and document conditions. The ICP expanded the qualifying residence-country list in June 2026 to include Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand and Canada alongside the previously listed countries. Do not assume that a tourist visa or short-term entry visa from one of these countries is equivalent to a qualifying residence permit.

Confirm current eligibility directly with ICP or the relevant emirate authority before purchasing non-refundable travel. Tourist visas are commonly arranged through eligible UAE airlines, hotels, licensed travel agencies or a UAE sponsor; the UAE Embassy does not issue ordinary tourist visas.

Documents and practical checks

  • Passport generally valid for at least six months from entry.
  • Approved visa or entry permission, where required.
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation details, where requested.
  • Evidence of funds or other supporting documents if required for the chosen route.
  • Travel insurance with medical and disruption cover; review exclusions for conflict and civil unrest.

Visa type, validity, permitted stay, fees and extension conditions differ. Use the official issuing channel for the final requirements; do not rely on a third-party fee table as a government quotation.

Indian visitor entry

Most Indian ordinary-passport travellers require a pre-arranged visit visa. Limited visa-on-arrival eligibility applies only when the traveller meets current ICP criteria, including qualifying foreign visa/residence status and passport validity. The June 2026 ICP expansion added six additional countries of residence for eligible Indian nationals. Verify the exact document category and conditions with ICP or Dubai’s official government portal; do not rely on old lists or third-party processing prices.

Student residence and employment

Students may be sponsored by an accredited educational institution or an eligible UAE-resident parent. The institution facilitates the residence process; immigration approval, medical screening, Emirates ID and insurance requirements apply. ICP guidance provides a 180-day grace period after completion of study, subject to the relevant rules.

Student training or employment requires the appropriate MoHRE permit and supporting conditions. A jobseeker visit visa may be available for 60, 90 or 120 days only to eligible bachelor’s-degree holders who meet the prescribed skill-level or recent top-500-university criteria and financial guarantee. It does not itself authorise employment.
