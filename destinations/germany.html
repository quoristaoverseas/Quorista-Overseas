01 · OVERVIEW

Germany at a glance

Germany combines research universities and universities of applied sciences. The right route depends on whether your school qualification gives direct entry (Hochschulzugangsberechtigung / HZB), whether a Studienkolleg and Feststellungsprüfung are needed, and whether your chosen course is taught in German or English. Berlin is the capital; Munich, Hamburg, Aachen, Heidelberg, Kassel and Giessen are among the cities students may compare. German is the official language; English is used widely in international programmes, especially at Master’s level.

Choosing the right route

Compare academic eligibility, teaching language, professional recognition, accommodation, local transport and overall cost. A city’s reputation alone does not determine whether a course or visit is suitable.

Rules, prices and travel conditions can change. Use official university, immigration and tourism sources before making commitments.

02 · STUDY ABROAD

Academic pathways in Germany

Shortlist by programme fit, language, tuition, recognition and student support—not reputation alone.

Study and scholarship guidance

Bachelor’s applicants should first check whether their school-leaving qualification is recognised for direct German university entry. Depending on the qualification and academic history, applicants may need a Studienkolleg and its final Feststellungsprüfung, or prior university study in their home country. Master’s applicants normally need a relevant recognised Bachelor’s degree and may face subject-specific credit, grade, aptitude or experience requirements. Many Master’s programmes are offered in English; English-taught Bachelor’s options are more limited. IELTS, TOEFL, TestDaF, DSH, Goethe and telc requirements vary by course—an MOI letter is not universally accepted.

  • Undergraduate: secondary school completion and subject prerequisites.
  • Postgraduate: relevant recognised bachelor’s degree; some courses require experience or portfolio.
  • Doctoral: research fit, proposal and supervisor availability.

Application documents & living

  • Passport, application form, certificates and transcripts.
  • Language evidence, statement of purpose and CV.
  • References, portfolio or research proposal where required.
  • Financial, medical, insurance and translated documents as specified.
  • APS certificate for most applicants using Indian-issued academic qualifications, unless an official exemption applies.
  • For non-direct Bachelor’s eligibility, investigate Studienkolleg and the Feststellungsprüfung (FSP) route.

Most public universities do not charge general tuition for many consecutive bachelor’s and master’s programmes, but students still pay a semester contribution. Important exceptions include non-EU tuition in Baden-Württemberg (generally €1,500 per semester) and fees set by some Bavarian universities; private and continuing-education programmes may cost much more. DAAD estimates living costs around €900–€1,200 monthly, while the 2026 study-visa financial proof is €11,904 for a year. Budget separately for deposits, insurance, travel and semester charges.

Scholarships

Funding routes include DAAD scholarships, university awards, Deutschlandstipendium and political or private foundations. DAAD calls differ by nationality, academic level, subject and intake; some require a separate scholarship application well before the university deadline. Deutschlandstipendium is normally €300 per month for selected students, subject to the university’s process. Funding is competitive and should never be assumed when preparing the visa budget.

DAAD scholarships for graduates and doctoral researchers are advertised through the DAAD scholarship database and country-specific calls. The Deutschlandstipendium is typically €300 per month for selected students, administered through participating universities. Political foundations may consider academic record, social engagement and their own selection criteria. Check nationality, degree level, subject, deadlines, coverage and renewal rules for each call. Admission does not guarantee a scholarship.

Student visa & admission

Applicants who need a visa should apply for the appropriate national visa for study before travelling. A state-recognised university admission is normally required; proof of funds, health insurance and language evidence may also be needed. The official 2026 funding reference for a study visa is at least €11,904 in a blocked account, or another accepted form such as a scholarship or declaration of commitment. For Indian-issued school or university qualifications, an APS certificate is generally required for admission/visa processing, subject to APS exemptions; check the current APS and mission rules before applying. After arrival, complete local registration and residence-permit formalities.

04 · TOURISM

Explore Germany

Germany rewards slower, region-by-region travel: combine historic cities, museums, architecture, castles, river landscapes and regional food. Berlin and Munich make a strong first trip; add Frankfurt and the Rhine Valley, Cologne, Hamburg, Dresden or Heidelberg when the available days and rail connections allow. May–September is generally comfortable for outdoor sightseeing, while December is popular for Christmas markets. Winter weather, event dates and regional conditions can affect plans.

Best season, flights & transport

May to September is generally favourable for longer days, outdoor activities and city touring; Christmas markets and winter sports make December to February attractive for a different trip. Direct flights from India commonly serve Frankfurt and Munich, with other gateways depending on airline and season. Germany’s long-distance trains connect major cities, while regional trains, S-Bahn, U-Bahn, trams and buses serve local journeys. Compare train fares and book popular long-distance routes early; allow extra transfer time.

Check weather and flight schedules for your exact dates. Compare baggage, arrival airports, transit visa needs and onward transfers. Use licensed local transport.

Tourist visa for Indian passport holders

Indian ordinary passport holders generally require a Schengen short-stay visa for tourism in Germany, normally for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Apply through the competent German mission/authorised service channel for your place of residence and main destination. Carry evidence of itinerary, accommodation, travel medical insurance, sufficient funds and return/onward travel. Check current fees, appointment routing and document rules before applying.

Hotels & budgets

Accommodation varies substantially by city and event calendar. In Berlin, compare well-connected districts beyond the immediate tourist core; in Munich, book early for trade fairs and Oktoberfest; in Hamburg and Heidelberg, compare station access with neighbourhood character. Hostels and guesthouses can lower costs, while apartment stays may add cleaning fees and city taxes. Check whether the room rate includes breakfast and local visitor taxes.

Indicative visitor budget (per person)

Use a broad planning range of roughly €85–€150 per person per day for a budget-to-mid-range visit, including an accommodation share, meals, local transport and ordinary sightseeing. Hostel beds and simple guesthouses may start around €35–€70 per night; many standard 3-star rooms can be around €100–€180 per room per night, but city, season, trade fairs, Oktoberfest and Christmas markets can push rates much higher. These are planning estimates, not live quotes.

For 8 days, use about €680–€1,200 per person on the ground as a broad starting estimate. Return flights from India, Schengen visa expenses, insurance, long-distance rail, car hire and premium attractions are additional. Book long-distance trains early and compare the full fare, including seat reservations and local transfers.

Germany's city-to-city costs differ; Munich and major event dates can exceed these ranges, while smaller university towns may be more economical. Treat the figures as a planning baseline, not a quotation.

Flexible five-day itinerary

Day 1: arrive in Berlin; explore the central district. Day 2: Reichstag area, Brandenburg Gate and Holocaust Memorial; reserve the Reichstag visit in advance. Day 3: Museum Island or East Side Gallery and neighbourhood time. Day 4: travel by rail to Munich; explore Marienplatz. Day 5: Munich museums, Nymphenburg Palace or English Garden. Day 6: optional Neuschwanstein Castle day trip (check official tickets and transfer time). Day 7: travel to Frankfurt; explore Römerberg and the riverfront. Day 8: depart from Frankfurt. This route is a suggested outline only; rail timetables, ticket availability and actual visit durations must be checked before booking.

Safety: Monitor official travel advisories and weather alerts, respect local laws and cultural norms, keep emergency contacts and insurance details accessible, and check activity-operator safety standards.

Current entry and study-permit checks

Official-source check for planning purposes. Reconfirm the rules for the traveller’s nationality, exact purpose and intended travel date before payment or departure.

Germany: use the 2026 financial and work rules. For a German study visa in 2026, the official federal information portal states that applicants can demonstrate living-cost funding through a blocked account of at least €11,904, a scholarship or a declaration of commitment. This is proof of funds, not the total cost of a degree or a guaranteed living-cost budget for every city. Under the current study residence rules, students may work up to 140 full days or 280 half days per year, or up to 20 hours per week. Indian ordinary passport holders need a visa for tourism; short visits use the Schengen C-visa route, while degree study normally requires the relevant national visa and subsequent residence formalities.
