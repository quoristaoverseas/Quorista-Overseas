01 · OVERVIEW

Slovakia at a glance

Slovakia is an EU and Schengen member in Central Europe, using the euro. Its higher-education system follows the Bologna structure and ECTS credit framework. Public and state institutions offer full-time Slovak-language programmes without tuition within the standard study period; foreign-language programmes may charge faculty-set fees. English-taught options exist in selected fields, but availability is programme- and intake-specific. Major student centres include Bratislava, Košice, Žilina, Nitra, Prešov and Banská Bystrica.

Why consider Slovakia?

Students can compare EU higher-education pathways, selected English-taught degrees, research-led institutions and lower-cost regional cities. Slovak-language public study can be tuition-free within the standard programme length, while English-language tuition varies by faculty. The country’s compact geography also makes it possible to combine historic cities, castles and mountain landscapes.

Do not assume that every listed subject is taught in English or that every programme accepts international applicants. Confirm the accredited programme, language, fees, recognition route and current intake directly with the faculty.

02 · STUDY ABROAD

Academic pathways in Slovakia

Shortlist by programme fit, language, tuition, recognition and student support—not reputation alone.

Study pathways

  • Language / preparatory study: university or approved-provider Slovak courses may prepare students for degree study. Confirm whether the specific course qualifies for a study-residence route; not every private language course does.
  • Bachelor’s (Bc.): commonly 3–4 years / 180–240 ECTS after recognised secondary education.
  • Master’s / Engineer (Mgr. / Ing.): commonly 1–2 years / 60–120 ECTS after a relevant bachelor’s degree.
  • Integrated long-cycle: medicine, dentistry and related regulated fields may have longer single-cycle structures.
  • Doctoral (PhD.): commonly 3–4 years, subject to research fit, supervisor and faculty selection.

These are typical structures, not guaranteed durations. The official programme and faculty admission notice determine eligibility, length, language and selection method.

Admission documents & recognition

  • Valid passport, application form, certificates and full transcripts.
  • Recognition / nostrification of foreign secondary or higher education, as required by the competent authority or university.
  • Official translations, legalisation or apostille where required.
  • English or Slovak language evidence accepted by the chosen faculty; some departments use interviews or entrance tests.
  • Motivation letter, CV, references, portfolio or research proposal where specified.
  • Application-fee receipt and any course-specific medical or practical documents.

Foreign qualifications must be assessed under the applicable recognition procedure. Do not assume a medium-of-instruction letter replaces a formal language test or qualification recognition.

English-taught programme checks

Comenius University Bratislava: a large comprehensive university with 13 faculties and options across medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, natural sciences, informatics, law, management, social sciences and humanities. English-taught options are faculty-specific. The university’s own applicant guidance should be read carefully: applicants should follow its direct faculty admissions process and confirm whether third-party representation is accepted before considering any external assistance.

Bratislava University of Economics and Business: its official accredited-programme list includes English-taught bachelor’s programmes such as Applied Economics, Finance, Banking and Insurance, Economics and Business Management, and International Economic Relations. English-taught master’s options include Applied Economics, Finance, Marketing and Trade Management, Tourism Management, General Management and Economic Diplomacy.

Slovak University of Technology (STU): selected English-taught engineering and computing programmes are available. STU publishes a joint English-taught Artificial Intelligence master’s programme with the Technical University of Košice for the 2027/28 academic year, with a stated annual fee of €7,000 and application deadline of 15 March 2027. This is a future intake; verify programme launch and eligibility before advising.

Scholarships

Slovakia has several distinct scholarship routes. The National Scholarship Programme is primarily for eligible short-term mobility, study, research, teaching or artistic stays and is not a general full-degree award. Separate government scholarships may support full-degree study for nationals of countries listed in a particular call. A further scheme supports talented international students under its own academic and testing criteria. Indian applicants appear among eligible nationalities for the National Scholarship Programme, but that does not make every full-degree scholarship available to Indian students. Confirm the current call, nationality, study level, eligible institution, selection criteria, funding amount and deadline before advising.

Student visa & admission

Non-EU/EEA students whose main purpose is eligible long-term study generally need temporary residence for the purpose of study; a Schengen tourist visa is not a substitute. Under the amendments effective 15 July 2026, the stated decision period for study-residence applications is now 90 days. The Slovak Ministry’s 15 August 2026 checklist identifies a valid travel document, admission confirmation (not older than 90 days) and recent criminal-record extracts; accommodation proof is required only for secondary-school students under that checklist. Additional requirements can depend on the application route and personal circumstances. Foreign documents may need apostille/superlegalisation and official Slovak translation. Start early and obtain a current individual checklist from the competent Slovak mission or Foreign Police.

Important planning point: Do not build a student’s intake or flight plan around the former 30-day expectation. Allow for the current 90-day study-residence decision period, plus appointment availability, document legalisation, translation and diplomatic-mail time where the application is filed abroad.

04 · TOURISM

Explore Slovakia

Plan a route around three distinct experiences: Bratislava’s Danube-side Old Town and castles, the High Tatras’ mountain lakes and cable-car resorts, and Košice’s Gothic historic centre and eastern-Slovak heritage. Spring to early autumn is generally best for city walks and hiking; winter is suited to skiing, subject to snow and mountain conditions. Vienna Airport is a common alternative gateway for Bratislava, around 60 km away, but include onward transfer time and check the correct Schengen entry route.

Best season, flights & local transport

Bratislava and lowlands: April–September is a comfortable period for outdoor cafés, riverside walks and city sightseeing. High Tatras: mid-June–October is generally suited to hiking, while January–March is the usual ski season; mountain weather, trail closures and cable-car operation are seasonal and must be checked locally.

From India, compare flights to Vienna (VIE) and Bratislava (BTS). Vienna often offers more connections; onward buses and trains reach Bratislava, but transfer times, fares and cross-border transit arrangements must be checked for the actual itinerary. Within Slovakia, ZSSK trains and intercity coaches connect Bratislava, Poprad and Košice. In Bratislava, use the IDS BK journey planner and buy a valid time-based ticket before travel; validate paper tickets as instructed.

Travel planning should not rely on fixed airline fares or journey durations. Flight prices, connections, rail timetables and local fares change by date and operator; verify live schedules and fares before travel.

Tourist visa for Indian passport holders

Indian ordinary passport holders require a Schengen short-stay (Type C) visa for tourism, normally for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Apply to Slovakia only when it is the main destination (or the applicable first-entry rule applies where no main destination can be determined). The Slovak Embassy in New Delhi’s current guidance directs Indian applicants to the authorised BLS International / VFS Global arrangements and identifies Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru as key centres; the embassy page also lists additional centre information, so check the live appointment portal for the applicant’s jurisdiction. Personal appearance is required. The published standard fee is €80 for adults and €40 for children aged 6–12; the stated decision period is 15 calendar days after the embassy receives a complete application, and can extend to 45 calendar days. Service fees, appointment availability and local submission arrangements may change.

Hotels & budgets

Bratislava has hostels and hotels around the Old Town, railway station and Petržalka; compare walking distance and tram/bus links. For the High Tatras, guesthouses and mountain pensions can be better value than resort hotels, but summer weekends and ski season book up early. Košice is a useful base for eastern Slovakia. Check whether a quoted room price includes breakfast, local tax and parking.

Indicative visitor budget (per person)

For early planning, use a broad allowance of around €45–€85 per person per day for a budget-to-mid-range trip where accommodation is shared or modest, meals are practical and activities are selective. A private hotel room, High Tatras resort stay, cable-car tickets, guided excursions and peak-season dates can raise the total substantially. Bratislava is generally the higher-cost city; regional towns and guesthouses may offer better value.

A 7-day trip could therefore require approximately €315–€595 per person on the ground as a first-pass planning range, before international flights, Schengen visa costs, insurance, premium excursions and any private transfers. Treat this as an indicative planning estimate—not a quotation or guaranteed live rate. Obtain date-specific hotel, flight, rail and attraction prices before confirming a package.

Keep a reserve for weather-related changes, mountain transport and activity tickets. Prices vary by season and location; verify current fares and accommodation quotes before confirming a trip.

05 · PLACES & EXPERIENCES

Places to consider

Bratislava

Old Town, Bratislava Castle, Danube promenade, UFO viewpoint and Devín Castle

High Tatras

Štrbské Pleso, Starý Smokovec, Tatranská Lomnica, alpine trails and cable cars

Košice

St. Elisabeth Cathedral, Main Street and eastern-Slovak culture

Spiš & Levoča

Spiš Castle, medieval heritage and nearby UNESCO-listed sites

Sample 7-day route: Bratislava · High Tatras · Košice

Day 1 – Bratislava: arrive, check in and take an easy Old Town walk. Day 2 – Bratislava: explore the castle, historic centre and Danube; consider Devín Castle if time allows. Day 3 – High Tatras: travel by rail towards Poprad / the Tatras and settle in near your chosen resort. Day 4 – High Tatras: choose a weather-appropriate lake walk, cable car or marked hiking route. Day 5 – Košice: travel east, visit St. Elisabeth Cathedral and the historic Main Street. Day 6 – Spiš / Levoča: take a planned day excursion, checking public transport or tour arrangements in advance. Day 7 – Departure: depart from a suitable regional airport or allow enough time to return to Vienna / Bratislava for the international flight.

This route requires intercity transfers and is not a no-travel-time itinerary. Confirm train schedules, accommodation locations, seasonal trail access and airport logistics before booking. A slower trip may omit Košice or the Tatras rather than attempting every region in one week.

Local food to try

  • Bryndzové halušky: potato dumplings with sheep’s cheese, often topped with bacon.
  • Kapustnica: traditional sauerkraut soup, with regional variations.
  • Vyprážaný syr: fried cheese commonly served with potatoes or fries.
  • Lokše: potato flatbreads served in sweet or savoury styles.

Menus, prices and ingredients vary. Ask about allergens and dietary needs before ordering.

Travel safety & practical notes

  • Emergency number: 112; police 158; ambulance 155; fire 150.
  • Mountain weather can change rapidly. Check official warnings, trail closures and cable-car operation before setting out.
  • Carry suitable footwear, water, layers and a charged phone; use marked trails and follow local mountain guidance.
  • Keep passport and insurance details secure, and use comprehensive travel medical insurance meeting Schengen requirements.

For current conditions and visitor information, use Slovakia Travel and the relevant regional tourism authority.
