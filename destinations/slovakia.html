Study and scholarship guidance

Slovakia has a substantial Slovak-language higher-education sector, alongside selected English-taught degrees and courses. The strongest immediately verifiable English-language pathways include medicine and selected science programmes at Comenius University, English-taught economics, finance, tourism management and international relations at Bratislava University of Economics and Business, and selected engineering/computing programmes at Slovak University of Technology and partner institutions.   • Undergraduate: secondary school completion and subject prerequisites.

English-taught programme checks

Comenius University Bratislava: its international applicant guidance describes 724 academic programmes across 13 faculties and confirms English-language study options including medicine, management and law, as well as selected natural sciences and European studies. Faculty deadlines and fees differ. The university explicitly says its admission process can be completed without external agencies and that it does not support agency-assisted applications; applicants should use the faculty’s own process.

Bratislava University of Economics and Business: its official accredited-programme list includes English-taught bachelor’s programmes such as Applied Economics, Finance, Banking and Insurance, Economics and Business Management, and International Economic Relations. English-taught master’s options include Applied Economics, Finance, Marketing and Trade Management, Tourism Management, General Management and Economic Diplomacy.

Slovak University of Technology (STU): selected English-taught engineering and computing programmes are available. STU publishes a joint English-taught Artificial Intelligence master’s programme with the Technical University of Košice for the 2027/28 academic year, with a stated annual fee of €7,000 and application deadline of 15 March 2027. This is a future intake; verify programme launch and eligibility before advising.

Scholarships

The National Scholarship Programme of the Slovak Republic is primarily a mobility scheme supporting study, research, artistic stays and teaching at Slovak higher-education institutions for eligible international applicants; it should not be presented as a general full-degree scholarship. For full-degree study, the Slovak Ministry of Education also lists government scholarships for partner countries and a separate national scholarship scheme for talented international students. These are distinct programmes with different eligibility rules and calls. Do not assume Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi or Nepali applicants are eligible: check the current country list, study level, subject restrictions, deadlines and application route before presenting any award as an option.

Student visa & admission

Third-country nationals admitted to eligible study programmes generally apply for temporary residence for the purpose of study. The Slovak Foreign Ministry lists proof of admission, a valid travel document and a criminal-record extract among the requirements; additional documents and translations/legalisation may apply. The permit may be granted for the period needed for study, up to the statutory maximum. Apply through the competent Slovak mission and confirm the current process for your nationality and institution.

04 · TOURISM

Explore Slovakia

Slovakia is best explored by combining Bratislava’s historic centre with one mountain or castle region. Late spring to early autumn is best for mountain hiking and outdoor trips; winter is suited to skiing and snow activities in the High Tatras. Bratislava is the main international gateway, while Vienna Airport is another practical arrival point; compare onward ground transfers and transit requirements. Bratislava’s compact centre is walkable, with trams, buses and trolleybuses; intercity trains and coaches connect regional cities.

Best season, flights & transport

Late spring through early autumn is generally the most comfortable period for city sightseeing and hiking, while winter is the season for skiing and snow activities in the High Tatras. Bratislava is the main gateway; Vienna Airport is a practical nearby alternative, but travellers must check their Schengen entry and onward-transfer arrangements. Use trains and coaches for regional travel; Bratislava’s city centre is compact and walkable.

Buy the correct time-based ticket before boarding and validate paper tickets immediately after entering the vehicle; an unvalidated ticket may be treated as invalid. Bratislava offers 30-, 60- and 90-minute tickets plus 24-, 72- and 168-hour tourist tickets. Check the current fare table and route planner before travel.

Tourist visa for Indian passport holders

Indian passport holders require a Schengen short-stay visa for tourism in Slovakia, normally for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. The Slovak Embassy in New Delhi states that Indian applicants should use the authorised BLS centres in Delhi, Mumbai or Bengaluru, with personal appearance required. The embassy’s published guidance lists the standard Schengen fee as €80; fees and local appointment arrangements can change. Apply to the country that is your main Schengen destination.
