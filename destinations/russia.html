02 · STUDY ABROAD

Study pathways in Russia

Russia has a large higher-education network, including public universities, specialist institutions and research-focused universities. The right option depends on the subject, teaching language, recognition requirements, budget and the exact programme—not simply the university name.

Understand the degree structure

  • Preparatory / Russian-language study: a foundation route for students who need to build Russian proficiency before entering a Russian-taught degree. Duration and structure vary by institution.
  • Bachelor’s degree: commonly four years, with subject prerequisites determined by the programme.
  • Specialist degree: a longer integrated professional qualification, often around five to six years in fields such as medicine and selected technical disciplines.
  • Master’s degree: commonly two years after a relevant bachelor’s degree.
  • Doctoral study: research-based postgraduate study, with admission dependent on academic background, research fit and university requirements.

Professional degrees and regulated fields may have additional requirements. Always check how the qualification will be recognised in the country where you intend to work.

English-taught or Russian-taught?

Both routes exist, but the availability of fully English-taught programmes differs significantly by university, subject and degree level.

  • English-taught: Russian proficiency may not be required for the degree itself, but the university can ask for an accepted English test, an interview or other proof.
  • Russian-taught: applicants generally need the required Russian proficiency or may first complete a university preparatory programme.
  • Mixed-language courses: check the language used in every year of study, clinical placements, laboratory work and examinations.
Important: IELTS 5.5, TOEFL scores and Medium of Instruction (MOI) letters are not universal admission standards for Russia. Each university and programme decides which evidence it accepts.
03 · ADMISSION

General admission requirements

These are starting points for planning. Final eligibility, document format, language evidence and selection methods must be confirmed with the university for the chosen intake.

Bachelor’s / Specialist

Usually requires completion of senior secondary education or an equivalent qualification. Mathematics, biology, chemistry or other subject prerequisites may apply depending on the course. Universities assess academic records and may use entrance tests, interviews or portfolio selection.

Master’s

Usually requires a recognised bachelor’s degree in a relevant or acceptable discipline, together with transcripts. Some programmes assess subject alignment, academic performance, a motivation letter, portfolio, interview or research interests.

Doctoral study

Usually requires a relevant specialist or master’s qualification, a suitable research background and evidence of research fit. A proposal, publications, interview or prospective supervisor confirmation may be required.

Documents applicants may need

  • Valid passport and university application form.
  • School or university certificates and complete transcripts.
  • Russian or English translations in the format requested by the institution.
  • Language proficiency evidence, where required.
  • Motivation letter, CV, references, portfolio or research proposal where relevant.
  • Medical, insurance, financial and other supporting documents requested for admission or the visa process.

Some qualifications may need apostille, legalisation or formal recognition. Do not arrange translations or legalisation until the university confirms the exact requirements.

Scholarships and government funding

International applicants may explore the Russian Government education quota, university scholarships and the Open Doors Russian Scholarship Project. Open Doors has Bachelor’s, Master’s, Doctoral and Postdoctoral tracks; successful candidates in eligible tracks may receive tuition-funded study through the government quota at participating institutions.

Tuition funding does not automatically mean that flights, accommodation, living costs, insurance and personal expenses are covered. Selection is competitive, subject areas and eligibility rules can change, and applicants must meet the language requirements of the chosen programme.

The 2026/27 Open Doors registration closed on 1 November 2026. Applicants should check the official platform for competition stages and the next cycle rather than rely on old deadlines.

Tuition and living expenses

Costs depend on the university, course, study language, city, accommodation type and scholarship status. Medical and specialist programmes may have a different fee structure from business, humanities or technical degrees.

For broad planning, monthly student living costs may be around RUB 40,000–70,000 in Moscow, RUB 30,000–50,000 in Saint Petersburg and RUB 20,000–35,000 in Kazan. Student visa overview

For applicants who require a visa, the usual study route begins after admission: the university arranges or supports the official invitation, the student completes the appropriate visa application through a Russian consular office, and then follows the university’s arrival, migration-registration and other legal formalities. Visa duration, entry count, extension and required documents depend on the individual case and current rules.

A tourist e-visa is not a student visa and should not be used as a substitute for the university-supported study route. The Russian MFA and the relevant consular office are the authorities for current visa instructions.

Student life and future planning

Moscow offers a large university and cultural environment but generally higher living costs. Saint Petersburg is a major academic and cultural centre. Kazan is a large regional university city with a multicultural character and potentially lower day-to-day costs. Other cities may suit students whose preferred programme is based there.

University dormitories can be more economical than private rentals, but availability, room conditions and allocation rules vary. Russian-language ability can make everyday life and local employment easier. Do not rely on part-time work to finance tuition or living expenses; check the current legal conditions and university guidance before accepting any work.

Before choosing a regulated profession, verify whether the degree and clinical or practical training will meet the licensing rules in the country where you plan to practise.

05 · INTERNATIONAL TOURISM

Explore Russia

From historic imperial cities and grand architecture to Arctic landscapes, Russia offers very different travel experiences across its regions. Distances are substantial, seasons are extreme in many areas and current travel conditions need careful checking before booking.

Moscow

Russia’s capital is known for Red Square, the Kremlin, St. Basil’s Cathedral and its elaborate metro stations. Visitors interested in history, architecture, museums, shopping and major city experiences often begin here.

Allow: around 2–3 days for key sights, depending on museum visits.

Saint Petersburg

A city of canals, palaces and museums. The Hermitage Museum, Peterhof, Nevsky Prospekt and the Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood are among its best-known attractions.

Allow: around 2–3 days; longer if visiting several museums and palace complexes.

Kazan

The capital of Tatarstan combines historic architecture and a distinctive Tatar cultural identity. The Kazan Kremlin, Kul Sharif Mosque and local food traditions are key points of interest.

Allow: around 1–2 days for a first visit.

Murmansk & Teriberka

Murmansk is a base for exploring the Kola Peninsula and Arctic coast. Seasonal experiences can include Northern Lights excursions, winter activities and trips towards Teriberka on the Barents Sea.

Plan carefully: weather, cloud cover, road conditions and restricted border areas can affect access and excursions.

Lake Baikal

One of the world’s best-known freshwater lake regions, associated with dramatic shorelines, nature and seasonal outdoor experiences. The best access and activities depend strongly on the time of year and local conditions.

Plan carefully: allow extra time for domestic travel and seasonal transfers.

Golden Ring & historic towns

A group of historic towns northeast of Moscow known for churches, monasteries, traditional architecture and heritage sites. These can add a slower-paced cultural element to a wider itinerary.

Plan carefully: check intercity transport and opening schedules before setting the route.

When to visit

  • Late spring to early autumn: often the most comfortable period for general sightseeing in Moscow and Saint Petersburg, with longer daylight hours.
  • Summer: popular for city breaks and outdoor visits; expect more visitors at major attractions.
  • Winter: very cold conditions in many regions, but seasonal scenery and winter activities may appeal to prepared travellers.
  • Murmansk aurora season: Northern Lights tours operate during the darker months, but sightings are never guaranteed; cloud cover and weather matter.

Getting around

Moscow and Saint Petersburg have extensive metro and public-transport networks. The Sapsan high-speed train connects the two cities, while domestic flights are often practical for longer distances such as travel to Murmansk. Local taxis and ride-hailing services may be available, but app access, payment and service conditions should be checked before arrival.

Flight schedules, rail timetables, fares and airport operations can change at short notice. Build flexibility into connections and avoid tight onward departures.

Russian food and dining

Popular dishes and food experiences include borscht (beetroot soup), pelmeni (filled dumplings), blini (thin pancakes) and beef stroganoff. In northern coastal areas, seafood such as cod and king crab may feature on menus.

Vegetarian options can be found in larger cities, including vegetable, mushroom and grain-based dishes, but soups and sauces may contain meat stock. Halal options are more readily found in major cities than in smaller or remote destinations. Travellers should confirm ingredients and certification directly with restaurants.

Accommodation and trip costs

Accommodation ranges from hostels and budget hotels to serviced apartments and premium hotels. Central locations near metro stations can reduce local travel time, while seasonal events and peak periods can increase room rates.

Daily spending depends on city, hotel category, transport, meals, attractions and exchange rates. Treat online budget figures as planning estimates only; compare live hotel and transport prices for the exact dates before confirming a trip.

06 · VISITOR ENTRY

Tourist visa overview for Indian passport holders

Indian citizens are eligible to apply for Russia’s unified electronic visa, subject to the current rules and authorised entry points.

Unified electronic visa (e-visa)

  • Single entry.
  • Valid for 120 days from issue; permitted stay is up to 30 days from entry.
  • Application window: no earlier than 86 days and no later than 4 days before the intended entry date.
  • Official processing time: up to 4 calendar days.
  • Travel medical insurance valid in Russia is required.
  • Entry and exit must be through an authorised border checkpoint.

The official portal states that an invitation, hotel booking confirmation or other proof of travel purpose is not required for the e-visa application. Always check the latest rules and checkpoint list before applying.

Regular visa route

A regular sticker visa may be more appropriate for a longer visit, multiple entries or a travel purpose that is outside the unified e-visa conditions. The required invitation or supporting documents, visa category, fees, appointment process and processing time depend on the application type and consular instructions.

Do not rely on third-party fee tables or old document checklists. Use the Russian Embassy / Visa Centre instructions applicable to the applicant’s place of residence and confirm the current checklist before submitting a passport.

Important travel and entry considerations

Russia requires additional due diligence. The security environment, transport operations, payment access and government advice can change quickly. Review the latest official information for your nationality and intended travel dates before making a commitment.

Security and government travel advice. As of 19 November 2026, the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office advises against all travel to Russia, citing security risks including drone attacks and short-notice restrictions on airspace and airport operations. This is the UK government’s advice to its nationals, not an Indian visa rule; travellers must consult the advice issued by their own government and assess insurance implications.

India-specific caution

The Indian Embassy’s 30 September 2026 advisory reiterates the Government of India’s warning to Indian nationals about unverified overseas job offers and recruitment linked to the Russian armed forces. This warning is especially important for anyone approached with employment, military-service or unusually high-pay offers. It should not be confused with an ordinary university admission offer; verify any institution, employer and visa purpose independently through official channels.

Payments, insurance and transport

International card networks and cross-border payment services may be restricted or unavailable in Russia. Confirm payment arrangements directly with accommodation providers and service operators before travel. Check that insurance explicitly covers Russia, the planned activities and any relevant government-advice exclusions. Keep flexible flight connections and an alternative departure plan because airport and airspace restrictions may occur with limited notice.

Restricted areas and local rules

Some border zones, military or sensitive facilities and other designated areas require special permission. This is particularly relevant when planning excursions in northern or border regions. Confirm permits, access restrictions, road conditions and local requirements with a reliable operator and competent authorities before setting out. Carry required identity and visa documents and follow local registration rules.

Separate study and tourist routes

A tourist e-visa is not valid for degree study. International students who require a visa must follow the university-supported study invitation and consular application process. Admission, scholarship awards, visa issuance and permission to enter are separate decisions made by the relevant institutions and authorities.
