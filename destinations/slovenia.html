01 · OVERVIEW

Slovenia at a glance

Slovenia is a compact Central European country known for Ljubljana, Alpine lakes and mountains, caves and a short Adriatic coastline. Students should check programme-level teaching language and non-EU tuition rules; visitors should plan regional transport as some popular nature sites are not directly served by rail.

Choosing the right route

Compare academic eligibility, teaching language, professional recognition, accommodation, local transport and overall cost. A city’s reputation alone does not determine whether a course or visit is suitable.

Rules, prices and travel conditions can change. Use official university, immigration and tourism sources before making commitments.

02 · STUDY ABROAD

Academic pathways in Slovenia

Shortlist by programme fit, language, tuition, recognition and student support—not reputation alone.

Study and scholarship guidance

Slovenia offers selected English-taught degree programmes, but the list is smaller than the full Slovenian-language catalogue and can change by academic year. The University of Ljubljana publishes an English-programme list that includes business and economics, finance, business informatics, international relations, European studies, public-sector governance and sustainable tourism management. Some programmes run in English only if there are enough applicants, so written confirmation from the faculty is essential before application.

  • Undergraduate: secondary school completion and subject prerequisites.
  • Postgraduate: relevant recognised bachelor’s degree; some courses require experience or portfolio.
  • Doctoral: research fit, proposal and supervisor availability.
  • Language: accepted test or local-language certificate where required.

Application documents & living

  • Passport, application form, certificates and transcripts.
  • Language evidence, statement of purpose and CV.
  • References, portfolio or research proposal where required.
  • Financial, medical, insurance and translated documents as specified.

For non-EU/EEA students without a qualifying exemption, published tuition ranges are generally €2,000–€11,000 per year for bachelor’s programmes, €2,000–€15,000 for master’s programmes and €2,000–€6,000 for doctoral programmes. Fees vary by institution and programme; confirm the exact price and exemption status directly. Compare university residence and private housing, deposits, utilities, meals and commute costs.

Scholarships

Scholarship availability for full-degree study is limited and nationality-specific. Slovenia’s 2026 public call for scholarships for foreign citizens is specifically intended for citizens of Western Balkan countries pursuing postgraduate master’s studies in selected fields; it is not a general scholarship for Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi or Nepali applicants. Other options may include bilateral agreements, Ad futura mobility funding and university-level awards, but each has its own eligible nationalities and purpose. Check the current call before promising any funding.

Student visa & admission

Non-EU students planning to stay longer than 90 days generally need a temporary residence permit for study. The official Slovenian guidance lists valid passport, health insurance, sufficient means, proof of enrolment and, depending on the case, a criminal-record certificate. Applications are normally submitted at a Slovenian diplomatic mission abroad unless the applicant is lawfully in Slovenia and eligible to apply locally. Register temporary residence after receiving the permit.

Explore Slovenia

Best season, flights & transport

Late spring to early autumn is the most versatile period for lakes, hiking and coastal visits. July and August are busier and accommodation near Lake Bled and the coast should be booked early. Winter suits skiing and snow activities in the Julian Alps. Ljubljana Jože Pučnik Airport is the main airport; some travellers compare nearby Trieste, Venice or Zagreb airports, checking cross-border transfers and Schengen entry. Buses are useful for many tourist routes; rail serves major towns but does not reach every lake or mountain attraction.

Tourist visa for Indian passport holders

Indian ordinary passport holders generally require a Schengen short-stay visa for tourism in Slovenia, normally up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Apply through Slovenia or the Schengen state that is your main destination; in some countries another Schengen mission represents Slovenia. The official Slovenian government advises applying at least 15 days before travel and no earlier than six months before the trip. Check the current consular representation and local checklist.
