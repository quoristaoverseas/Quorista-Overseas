02 · STUDY ABROAD

Academic pathways in France

Shortlist by programme fit, language, tuition, recognition and student support—not reputation alone.

Study and scholarship guidance

French higher education follows the Licence–Master–Doctorat (LMD) structure: Licence is usually 3 years (180 ECTS), Master 2 years (120 ECTS), and a doctorate commonly at least 3 years. Engineering diplomas, BUT and specialist-school qualifications have their own structures. Campus France’s October 2026 catalogue lists more than 1,600 programmes wholly or partly taught in English; verify the teaching-language share and French requirements for internships or regulated professions.

  • Licence: secondary-school qualification recognised for higher-education entry; DAP, Parcoursup, Études en France or direct application may apply.
  • Master: relevant recognised Bachelor’s degree; prerequisites, grades, experience or portfolio vary.
  • Doctorat: relevant Master’s-level qualification, research proposal and suitable supervisor/doctoral-school route.
  • French-medium courses commonly require B2 or higher; English-medium courses set their own IELTS, TOEFL or equivalent requirements. MOI acceptance is institution-specific.

Application documents & living

  • Passport, application form, certificates and transcripts.
  • Language evidence, statement of purpose and CV.
  • References, portfolio or research proposal where required.
  • Financial, medical, insurance and translated documents as specified.

Request a current written fee breakdown, including application, registration, insurance, examination and laboratory charges. Compare university residence and private housing, deposits, utilities, meals and commute costs.

Scholarships and financial support

France Excellence Eiffel: competitive Master’s and doctoral scholarship. From January 2026, the monthly allowance is €1,200 at Master’s level and €2,000 at doctoral level, plus certain services. The programme does not itself pay tuition fees; a French institution submits the nomination. Also check French government awards through the Embassy/Campus France India, institutional grants, Erasmus+ and Campus Bourses. Eligibility and deadlines vary; funding is not guaranteed.

Student visa for applicants from India

For full-time study lasting more than 90 days, the usual route is a long-stay student visa (VLS-TS “Étudiant”), subject to programme and applicant category. Campus France India states that Études en France (EEF) is the first step for long-stay student visa applicants in its jurisdiction; follow its current checklist, interview and pre-consular instructions before submitting through France-Visas. After arrival, validate the VLS-TS online within the required period.

Financial proof: from 1 August 2026, the minimum is €877.50 per month. This is a visa threshold, not a realistic full living-cost budget. Student visa holders may generally work up to 964 hours per year, subject to conditions; do not rely on employment to fund tuition or essential expenses. Campus France India lists an EEF processing fee of ₹20,000 from 1 August 2026; visa fees and service charges are separate.

04 · TOURISM

Explore France

France offers major cultural, food, heritage and coastal experiences. Build an itinerary around regions and realistic rail journeys rather than trying to cover the whole country in a short visit.

When to visit, flights & intercity travel

Paris and northern France: April–June and September–October are often comfortable for sightseeing. July–August bring larger crowds and higher hotel demand; winter is colder and quieter. French Riviera: May–June and September–October can offer warm weather with less intense peak-season crowds; July–August are busy and expensive.

Air France and Air India have operated non-stop India–Paris services on selected routes; schedules, operating days, baggage rules and fares change. Compare Delhi and Mumbai departures with one-stop alternatives. Paris–Lyon is commonly around two hours by high-speed train; Lyon–Nice varies by service and connection. Check exact date-specific rail schedules before confirming a multi-city itinerary.

Tourist visa for Indian passport holders

Indian ordinary passport holders generally need a Schengen short-stay visa (Type C) before travelling to France. It normally permits up to 90 days in any 180-day period across the Schengen Area. Apply to France when it is the main destination by length of stay, subject to Schengen rules.

Use the France-Visas assistant for the personal checklist. Typical evidence includes passport, application, itinerary, accommodation, proof of funds and employment/business or study status, return travel plan and Schengen-compliant medical insurance. The standard short-stay fee is €90 for adults; INR fee and VFS service charge are confirmed at application. France-Visas lists VFS centres across India, including Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Chandigarh, Chennai, Delhi, Goa, Hyderabad, Jaipur, Jalandhar, Kochi, Kolkata, Mumbai, Puducherry and Pune. Consulates and the Embassy—not VFS—make visa decisions. Apply well ahead; processing estimates are not guarantees.

Accommodation & local transport

Paris: choose accommodation near a Metro/RER station and compare journey times; central districts can be expensive. Lyon: Presqu’île and Part-Dieu offer different sightseeing and rail-access trade-offs. Nice: central districts and the station area are practical bases; Riviera prices rise during summer and major events.

Compare hostels, guesthouses, chain hotels and private stays. Check tourist tax, breakfast, cancellation terms, room occupancy and whether rates include all charges. Confirm current ticket validity and airport-transfer fares on official local transport sites instead of relying on old “t+” or pass prices.

Attractions, food & visitor budget

Paris: Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Musée d’Orsay, Arc de Triomphe, Montmartre and Versailles. Lyon: Vieux Lyon, traboules, Fourvière Basilica, Roman theatres and traditional bouchon dining. Nice: Promenade des Anglais, Old Nice, Castle Hill and museums; Monaco and Cannes can be optional day excursions. Try regional dishes such as beef bourguignon, Lyonnaise quenelles and Niçoise specialities.

Indicative daily planning bands (per person)

Budget-conscious travel may require roughly €100–€150/day; comfortable mid-range travel can be around €240–€410/day; premium travel can exceed €740/day. These are broad planning bands, not live quotes. They vary with room-sharing, city, season, dining and paid attractions. International flights, visa charges, insurance and intercity rail should be budgeted separately.

Suggested eight-day route: Paris, Lyon & Nice

Day 1 – Paris: arrival and Seine-side walk. Day 2: Eiffel Tower and nearby landmarks. Day 3: Louvre or Musée d’Orsay, then Montmartre. Day 4 – Lyon: high-speed train, Vieux Lyon and traboules. Day 5: Fourvière, Roman theatres and a local food market or bouchon. Day 6 – Nice: rail journey, check-in and Promenade des Anglais. Day 7: Old Nice, Castle Hill and optional Monaco excursion. Day 8: departure from Nice Côte d’Azur Airport.

This is a planning framework, not a confirmed transport schedule. Check actual train times, transfer time, attraction availability and flight routing. If the return flight departs Paris, allow for the return journey or price an open-jaw ticket separately.

Safety: Monitor official travel advisories and weather alerts, respect local laws and cultural norms, keep emergency contacts and insurance details accessible, and check activity-operator safety standards.

Current entry and study-permit checks

Official-source check for planning purposes. Reconfirm the rules for the traveller’s nationality, exact purpose and intended travel date before payment or departure.

France: update the 2026 student visa financial threshold. Campus France confirms differentiated public-institution registration fees of €2,902 per year for Licence, €3,950 for Master and €398 for doctoral study in 2026–27 for non-EU students within the differentiated-fee regime. Exemptions may apply; private institutions and grandes écoles set their own fees. Campus France India confirms a minimum student-visa resource threshold of €877.50 per month from 1 August 2026. This is a visa minimum, not a complete living-cost budget. Treat this as the minimum financial-resource requirement, not a complete estimate of actual living costs in Paris or other cities. Applicants for long-stay study should follow the Études en France procedure where applicable and then the France-Visas process; the exact route depends on the programme and applicant category. Indian ordinary passport holders need a Schengen short-stay visa for tourism, separate from a long-stay student visa.
