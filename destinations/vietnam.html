02 · STUDY ABROAD

Study in Vietnam: pathways and universities

Vietnam offers a mix of international campuses, private universities and public institutions. English-medium options exist, but the language, degree-awarding institution and international eligibility must be checked course by course.

Academic pathways

  • Undergraduate: bachelor’s programmes commonly take around 3–4 years, depending on the institution and discipline.
  • Postgraduate: master’s programmes often take 1–2 years; relevant prior study and, for some courses, professional experience may be required.
  • Doctoral: research-led admission normally depends on a suitable prior qualification, research proposal and supervisor or faculty fit.
  • Preparatory study: selected institutions offer English, foundation or Vietnamese-language preparation routes. These do not automatically guarantee progression.

Vietnamese-taught degrees require suitable Vietnamese proficiency. For English-medium courses, check the university’s accepted tests and minimum scores. Do not assume that a Medium of Instruction letter is accepted everywhere.

Typical application documents

  • Passport and application form.
  • School or university certificates and transcripts.
  • Accepted English or Vietnamese language evidence.
  • Statement of purpose, CV and references where required.
  • Portfolio, interview, entrance assessment or research proposal for selected programmes.
  • Certified translations, financial evidence and other documents requested by the institution or immigration authority.

Entry grades, subject prerequisites, age conditions, application windows and document formats vary. Ask for the current international admissions guide for the exact intake.

Examples of English-medium opportunities and funding

RMIT Vietnam: offers international full-degree programmes in several disciplines. Its published 2026 schedule lists indicative annual tuition of VND 375,840,000 for many standard undergraduate programmes (around US$14,345), while postgraduate fees vary by course and credit load. Compulsory insurance and other charges may apply; fees are billed in VND and should be checked for the intended intake.

VinUniversity: its published policy states a 35% tuition subsidy for students enrolling from 2025 to 2030 for the duration of study, alongside competitive merit scholarships. The subsidy and merit awards have their own terms; check the programme and award conditions directly.

UEH: publishes full-time international scholarship routes across undergraduate, master’s and doctoral levels. Its current policy describes tuition support, dormitory accommodation and a VND 2.6 million monthly living allowance during official semesters for selected awardees. Academic, language, selection and availability conditions apply; the scholarship is competitive and not automatic.

Fulbright University Vietnam: uses a holistic admissions process and publishes application-cycle information and financial-aid details separately. Review the current cycle dates and eligibility rather than relying on a previous year’s deadline.

HCMUT: publishes specific international undergraduate admission and English-language requirements. Confirm whether the intended course is delivered in English and whether the relevant international intake is accepting applications.

Ton Duc Thang University (TDTU): its 2026–2027 admissions information lists 11 undergraduate majors taught fully in English, including business, finance, software engineering and computer science. The university lists IELTS 5.0 or an accepted equivalent as the usual English-language threshold and an estimated tuition fee of around VND 80 million per academic year for its English-taught undergraduate route in 2026. It also publishes full and partial tuition or dormitory scholarships for eligible international applicants. These are programme-specific estimates and competitive awards, not guaranteed admission or funding. TDTU separately lists mixed Vietnamese-English programmes; applicants should not confuse these with fully English-taught courses.

Student visa

International students generally need a study-purpose DH visa. The host university’s international office normally coordinates or sponsors the relevant immigration paperwork after admission. The exact steps, timing, fees and supporting documents are institution-specific. A tourist e-visa is not a substitute for the correct study permission.

Confirm the process with the university before paying deposits or booking travel. Students must also comply with local temporary-residence registration and visa-extension requirements.

Plan the full cost—not just tuition

Compare tuition, compulsory insurance, registration charges, accommodation, utilities, food, transport, visa expenses and travel. Private international campuses can cost substantially more than local public-university routes. Scholarships may reduce tuition but do not always cover living costs or travel.

Part-time work and post-study employment should not be treated as guaranteed sources of funding. Check current immigration and employment rules before relying on work income.

04 · INTERNATIONAL TOURISM

Discover Vietnam

From historic cities and limestone bays to beaches, food culture and heritage towns, Vietnam offers several distinct travel regions. A first visit is often easier to plan around one or two regions rather than rushing the entire country.

Where to go

  • Hanoi: Old Quarter, Hoan Kiem Lake, historic temples, museums and Vietnamese food culture.
  • Ha Long Bay: limestone karsts, boat cruises and island scenery; choose licensed operators and check weather-related cancellations.
  • Da Nang: My Khe Beach, Marble Mountains and access to central Vietnam.
  • Hoi An: preserved old-town architecture, lantern-lit streets, markets and nearby heritage sites.
  • Ho Chi Minh City: museums, markets, architecture, food and southern day trips.
  • Phong Nha / Ninh Binh: caves, river landscapes and nature-focused excursions.

When to visit

Vietnam does not have one nationwide best season. Weather differs between regions:

  • North (Hanoi, Ha Long Bay): generally pleasant in March–May and October–December; summer can be hot, wet and storm-affected.
  • Central coast (Da Nang, Hoi An): often best from February to August. Heavy rain, typhoons and flooding are more likely around September–December, especially October–November.
  • South (Ho Chi Minh City, Mekong Delta, Phu Quoc): generally drier from November/December to April; wetter from May to October.

Tet (Lunar New Year) is a major holiday period. Transport and accommodation can book out and some services operate reduced hours.

Getting there and around

Selected direct flights connect Indian cities with Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City; routes, airlines, frequencies and fares change by season. Compare the full itinerary, baggage allowance, transit conditions and arrival airport before booking. Domestic flights are convenient for long distances; trains and intercity buses provide alternatives on selected routes. In cities, use reputable ride-hailing services or licensed taxis, agree the fare where appropriate and keep an eye on belongings in traffic.

For a multi-city trip, allow time for airport transfers and weather disruption. Do not assume every domestic route operates daily or at the same frequency year-round.

Accommodation and everyday costs

Vietnam offers hostels, guesthouses, city hotels, boutique stays and beach resorts. Prices vary significantly by city, season, location and booking lead time. Compare recent reviews, cancellation terms, transport access and whether taxes or service charges are included.

Street food and local eateries can be inexpensive, while international restaurants, cruises, resorts and organised tours increase the budget. Carry Vietnamese đồng for markets and small purchases; cards are more widely accepted at hotels, malls and larger restaurants. Build a separate allowance for flights, visa, insurance, attraction tickets and excursions.

Tourist visa for Indian passport holders

Indian ordinary passport holders generally need a visa before travelling to Vietnam. The official Immigration Department e-visa system allows eligible foreign nationals to apply for an e-visa for up to 90 days, with single or multiple entry. Published government fees are US$25 for single entry and US$50 for multiple entry; these fees are non-refundable if an application is refused. The official portal lists a standard processing period of 3 working days, but this is not a guaranteed decision time. Apply well in advance, verify the approved entry/exit port and ensure the approved dates and passport details match the trip.

Visa on arrival should not be treated as a routine alternative; it is generally limited to qualifying cases with prior authorisation. Visa-free Phu Quoc arrangements have specific routing and stay conditions and should not be assumed to cover onward travel elsewhere in Vietnam.

Travel smart and stay prepared

  • Check the latest travel advisories and weather warnings before departure.
  • Use travel insurance that covers medical care and weather-related disruption.
  • Drink bottled or properly treated water and take normal food and mosquito precautions.
  • Use helmets and avoid unfamiliar self-drive motorbike routes; confirm licence and insurance requirements before riding.
  • Keep valuables secure around roads and crowded markets; use reputable transport providers.
  • Respect local laws and customs. E-cigarettes and heated tobacco products are prohibited under Vietnamese law; do not carry them.

Emergency numbers: Police 113 · Fire 114 · Ambulance 115. Keep your embassy contact details and insurance assistance number accessible.
