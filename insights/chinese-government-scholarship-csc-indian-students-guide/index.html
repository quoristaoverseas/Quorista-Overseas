Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC): A Guide for Indian Students

The Chinese Government Scholarship offers routes to study in China at different academic levels. Understand the CSC system, university selection, funding terms and application checks before applying.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 11 min readUpdated: 25 November 2026

The Chinese Government Scholarship (often called CSC) supports international students pursuing eligible study or research in China. The programme is associated with the China Scholarship Council and is offered through different channels, including embassy-related and university-based routes. Available degree levels, fields, quotas and funding terms depend on the specific scholarship category and annual call.

For Indian students, the most important task is to identify the correct official application route. A university may advertise a scholarship, but the student must determine whether it is a Chinese Government Scholarship, a provincial award, a university scholarship or another funding scheme. These awards can have different eligibility rules and benefits.

Important distinction: “CSC scholarship” is often used broadly online. Always verify the scholarship category, agency, application channel and funding schedule in the official notice. Do not assume every scholarship in China is administered by CSC.

1. What is the Chinese Government Scholarship?

The Chinese Government Scholarship programme supports international education and exchange in China. Applications may be routed through designated authorities or directly through participating Chinese universities, depending on the category. The CampusChina portal provides official scholarship and university information, while individual universities publish their programme-specific instructions.

There are different scholarship types and application channels. Some are full scholarships; others may have different levels of support. The exact category code or agency number requested in the application is important and must match the relevant call.

2. Study levels and scholarship routes

Government scholarship opportunities may be available for undergraduate, master's and doctoral study, as well as general scholar or senior scholar categories. The academic level, age limit, qualification and duration requirements differ. Some programmes are taught in Chinese and may include language preparation; others may be offered in English or require a specified language level.

Embassy or bilateral route

Some scholarship calls are administered through the applicant's home-country authority or diplomatic channel. The applicant should follow the relevant official notice for Indian citizens and verify where documents must be submitted. Do not infer that a route is open merely because another country's embassy has published a call.

University route

Many Chinese universities accept applications for scholarship categories through their international student offices. The university may require a separate admission application, a pre-admission letter or a supervisor's acceptance for certain doctoral programmes. Check whether the university is authorised to recommend candidates under the scholarship category being advertised.

3. What may the scholarship cover?

A full Chinese Government Scholarship package may include tuition, accommodation or accommodation subsidy, a monthly living stipend and comprehensive medical insurance. The specific package depends on the category and official rules. International airfare is not automatically included in every call; some official notices expressly state that international travel expenses are not covered.

Check the official notice for the exact funding items. Ask whether accommodation is provided in a university dormitory or paid as a subsidy, whether the stipend begins after registration, whether tuition includes all compulsory fees and whether insurance covers the services you may need. A full scholarship should not be assumed to cover flights, visa fees, medical examinations, document authentication, deposits or personal expenses.

4. Eligibility and academic requirements

Common eligibility conditions include non-Chinese citizenship, good health, the required prior qualification, age limits and academic suitability. For example, a master's applicant normally needs a bachelor's degree, while a doctoral applicant normally needs a master's degree. The exact age cut-offs and qualification deadlines must be read from the current programme notice.

Language requirements depend on the language of instruction. Chinese-taught programmes may require HSK results or a preparatory language route. English-taught programmes may request IELTS, TOEFL, another accepted certificate or proof of prior English-medium education, depending on the university. Do not assume that a medium-of-instruction letter is accepted by every institution.

Some undergraduate scholarship routes may introduce additional entrance or assessment requirements. The relevant annual notice and university admission rules should be checked carefully, as these can change between intakes.

5. How to apply through the official process

  1. Identify the scholarship category and whether the application is through an embassy, designated authority or university.
  2. Search the official CampusChina university and programme information and shortlist suitable institutions.
  3. Read the selected university's admission requirements, language policy, programme duration and scholarship instructions.
  4. Contact the university through its published international admissions channel if a pre-admission letter or supervisor confirmation is required.
  5. Register in the official online application system specified by the notice and complete the correct application form.
  6. Enter the correct agency or programme information, upload the required documents and submit before the official deadline.
  7. Track the university or authority's communications and complete any additional admission, medical or visa steps.

Some current notices require undergraduate applicants to complete an additional academic assessment. The requirement, accepted test, subjects and deadline should be verified in the relevant call and with the university.

6. Documents applicants may need

  • Passport and identity information.
  • Highest qualification certificate and academic transcripts.
  • Study plan or research proposal, depending on level.
  • Recommendation letters for graduate applicants where required.
  • Language proficiency evidence matching the programme's teaching language.
  • Foreigner physical examination form or other health documentation where requested.
  • Police clearance or non-criminal record certificate if required by the current notice.
  • Pre-admission letter, portfolio, supervisor acceptance or other programme-specific evidence where applicable.

Document validity periods matter. Medical reports, police certificates and language results may have time limits. Follow the official form, translation and certification rules rather than preparing documents based on an unrelated university's checklist.

7. How to verify a genuine scholarship offer

Confirm the scholarship name and category in the official notice. Verify that the university is recognised and that the intended programme is open to international students. Use the official university domain and the application portal named in the call. If an agent claims that a scholarship is guaranteed or asks you to pay a large fee to obtain a government award, treat this as a warning sign.

Read the final admission and scholarship documents separately. An admission offer does not always mean that the scholarship has been awarded. A scholarship nomination may also be subject to final approval, registration and immigration requirements. Keep copies of all official correspondence.

8. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Confusing CSC with provincial or university-specific scholarships.
  • Using an agency number or category from an old or unrelated notice.
  • Assuming airfare is included in every full scholarship.
  • Applying to a programme without confirming language requirements.
  • Ignoring pre-admission, supervisor or extra assessment requirements.
  • Assuming an English-medium letter is accepted by every university.
  • Believing a scholarship offer guarantees a visa or entry clearance.

Frequently asked questions

Can Indian students apply for CSC?

Indian citizens may be eligible for specific Chinese Government Scholarship calls if India is included and they meet the relevant requirements. Check the current official notice for the applicable route.

Does CSC cover international flights?

Not necessarily. Some full scholarship packages cover tuition, accommodation, stipend and insurance but exclude international travel. Read the exact call before budgeting.

Can I study in English?

Some Chinese universities offer English-taught programmes. Language evidence and programme availability must be checked with the university and scholarship notice.

Do I need a pre-admission letter?

Some categories or universities require or recommend one, while others may not. Follow the exact application route and university instructions.

Is a CSC scholarship guaranteed through an agency?

No. Scholarship selection is made through the official process. No private intermediary can guarantee a government scholarship or visa outcome.
