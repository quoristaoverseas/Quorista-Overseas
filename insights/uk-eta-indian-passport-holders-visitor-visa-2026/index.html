UK ETA for Indian Passport Holders in 2026: Do Indians Need an ETA or a Visitor Visa?

A practical clarification of the UK's digital travel-permission rules, the difference between an ETA and a visitor visa, and what Indian travellers should check before flying.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 10 min readUpdated: 25 November 2026Reviewed: 25 November 2026

Searches for “UK ETA for Indians” have increased as the United Kingdom has expanded its Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) scheme and enforced digital permission to travel. The important distinction is nationality: an ETA is generally for people who do not need a visa for a short visit to the UK. Indian ordinary passport holders are visa nationals for visitor travel and should not treat an ETA as a substitute for a UK visa.

Answer for most Indian tourists: An Indian citizen travelling on an ordinary Indian passport for a holiday, family visit or other permitted short visit normally needs a UK Standard Visitor visa before travelling. The UK ETA scheme does not convert Indian passport holders into visa-free visitors.

1. Do Indian passport holders need a UK ETA in 2026?

For an ordinary Indian passport holder travelling to the UK as a visitor, the usual route is a Standard Visitor visa, not an ETA. The UK ETA is a digital travel authorisation for eligible nationalities that can visit the UK without obtaining a visitor visa in advance. The UK Home Office's ETA nationality list identifies which nationalities can use the scheme.

That means an Indian traveller should begin by checking the UK visa requirement for their nationality and purpose. Do not pay an intermediary to obtain an ETA on the assumption that it will replace the visitor visa. An ETA approval, if a person is eligible for one under another nationality, is not a UK visa and does not confer permission to work or live in the UK.

People with dual nationality or a different passport may have a different result. They must check the rules for the passport they will actually use to travel and ensure their carrier and UK border records match their travel document and immigration status.

2. What is the UK Electronic Travel Authorisation?

An ETA is a digital permission to travel to the UK for eligible visitors who do not normally need a visa for a short visit. It is linked electronically to the passport used in the application. It is not a visa, does not guarantee entry and does not replace the border officer's decision.

The UK Home Office announced that from 25 February 2026, eligible non-visa nationals would be required to hold an ETA or another valid form of permission before travel. The published Home Office notice stated a fee of £16 at that time and described an ETA as normally valid for two years or until the passport expires, whichever comes first. Fees and validity conditions can change, so check the live GOV.UK page before making any application.

The scheme also matters for certain airside or landside transit situations. The Home Office announcement says ETA is required for visitors who travel to the UK to take connecting flights and go through passport control. Transit requirements depend on the actual route, nationality and whether the traveller passes UK border control.

3. What should Indian travellers apply for?

If you hold an Indian ordinary passport and plan to visit the UK for tourism, visiting family or friends, certain business visitor activities, or another permitted Standard Visitor purpose, check the Standard Visitor visa route. The visa application is separate from an ETA application.

Before applying, identify the purpose and duration of the visit and check whether the activity is permitted under the visitor route. A visitor visa does not normally permit employment, taking a job, accessing public funds or living in the UK through frequent or successive visits. Some business activities are permitted, but the detailed visitor rules should be checked rather than assuming that every work-related activity is allowed.

Applicants should prepare truthful evidence that explains their trip and circumstances. Depending on the case, relevant evidence can include:

  • A clear travel purpose, proposed dates and itinerary.
  • Information about accommodation and who will pay for the trip.
  • Financial evidence showing how the visit will be funded and that the funds are genuinely available.
  • Employment, business, study or other personal circumstances relevant to the application.
  • Evidence of the applicant's plans and reasons to leave the UK at the end of the visit.
  • Details of any previous immigration history or refusals, disclosed accurately where required.

There is no universal bank-balance figure that guarantees a UK visitor visa. The evidence should make sense in light of the trip's cost, the applicant's income and circumstances, and any sponsor's genuine ability and willingness to pay.

4. What about UK transit and connecting flights?

Do not assume that a short connection automatically means no UK immigration permission is needed. The key question is whether the passenger remains airside or passes through UK border control, alongside the applicable transit-visa rules for their nationality and circumstances.

If you need to collect baggage, change airports, leave the international transit area or otherwise pass through border control, a different permission may be required. Indian nationals should check the UK transit visa guidance and any applicable exemptions before booking. Ask the airline to confirm the operational itinerary, but rely on GOV.UK for the immigration rule.

A valid visa or ETA requirement may also depend on the exact route, destination, ticketing and documents held. Check every transit country, not only the final destination. An airline's willingness to sell a ticket is not proof that you meet the immigration requirements.

5. How to avoid ETA-related mistakes and scams

  • Check the nationality list on GOV.UK before assuming you qualify for an ETA.
  • Use the official GOV.UK service or official UK ETA app when you are eligible; do not trust sponsored search results merely because they appear first.
  • Do not submit an ETA application as a replacement for a visa if your passport nationality requires a visa.
  • Check whether the application is for a visitor visa, transit visa or ETA—the names and legal permissions are different.
  • Use the same passport consistently for the application and travel, and keep passport details up to date.
  • Check current fees, processing information and carrier requirements close to departure.
  • Be cautious of websites that promise guaranteed approval, priority government decisions or a special ETA route for Indian ordinary passport holders.

Indian passport holders: start with the visa requirement, not the ETA application

The Home Office's current visa-requirements list includes India among nationalities that need a visa to enter or transit the UK. The ETA National List is a separate list for nationalities eligible for ETA travel. Therefore, an Indian ordinary passport holder should normally apply for the appropriate UK visa for a visit rather than an ETA.

Before paying any application fee, use the official GOV.UK visa checker for the exact nationality, purpose and journey. Do not use an intermediary that promises an ETA as a substitute for a visa-required route.

If you hold more than one passport

ETA eligibility is based on the nationality and passport used for travel. A person who also holds a passport from an ETA-eligible country may have a different travel route from someone travelling only on an Indian passport. However, you must follow the rules for the passport and status you actually use, and the airline's document checks must match that route.

  • Check whether the second nationality is on the current ETA National List.
  • Use the same passport consistently for the ETA application, airline booking and travel where the system requires it.
  • If you hold existing UK immigration permission, check whether you need an ETA at all; GOV.UK says people who already have permission to be in the UK do not need an ETA.
  • Do not assume that an Indian passport plus a foreign residence card makes the traveller eligible for ETA. Residence in an ETA-eligible country is not the same as holding an eligible nationality.

Transit through the UK: airside and landside are different

Transit rules depend on whether you pass UK border control. A traveller who remains airside may be assessed under Direct Airside Transit Visa rules; a traveller who passes border control, collects baggage and checks in again, changes airport or stays landside may need a different visa. Indian nationals are included in the UK visa-required nationality list, but specific exemptions can apply depending on the traveller's documents and circumstances.

Before booking a connection, check the official UK transit visa guidance, the current carrier requirements and the exact airport/terminal itinerary. Ask the airline whether the connection is genuinely airside and whether baggage is checked through. Do not rely on the phrase “international transit” in a booking confirmation as proof that no visa is needed.

What a Standard Visitor visa does—and does not—allow

A Standard Visitor visa is for permitted short visits such as tourism, visiting family or friends and certain other activities listed in the rules. It does not normally permit taking employment, living in the UK through frequent successive visits, or using visitor status as a substitute for a work or long-term study route. Check the activity list and conditions before applying.

Use the official Standard Visitor visa guidance to confirm the correct category, permitted activities, application process and current fee. If your purpose is study, work, marriage or a long-term stay, investigate the relevant route instead of applying as a visitor.

A safe pre-booking checklist for Indian travellers

  1. Confirm the passport you will use and its nationality.
  2. Use GOV.UK's visa checker for your purpose and travel dates.
  3. If a visa is required, apply for the correct visa category and allow time for processing.
  4. If transiting, check whether the route is airside or landside and whether a transit visa or exemption applies.
  5. Check that the visa or ETA is linked to the passport used for travel and that the passport details are correct.
  6. Recheck the rules before departure because nationality lists and digital permission requirements can change.

Frequently asked questions

Can an Indian citizen get a UK ETA instead of a visitor visa?

An Indian ordinary passport holder normally needs a UK Standard Visitor visa for a visit. ETA is for eligible visa-exempt nationalities and is not a substitute for a visa-required route.

What if I have both an Indian and another country's passport?

Eligibility depends on the nationality and passport used for travel, as well as any status or visa held. Check the current UK rules for the exact passport you intend to use and keep your travel documents consistent.

Does a UK ETA guarantee entry?

No. ETA is permission to travel for eligible visitors, not a guarantee of admission. Border officials still assess whether the traveller meets the entry requirements.

Do Indians need an ETA for a UK airport connection?

Indian nationals should check the applicable transit visa rules. ETA is relevant to eligible non-visa nationals, while Indian passport holders may need a transit visa depending on whether they pass border control and whether an exemption applies.

Can I work in the UK with an ETA or Standard Visitor visa?

An ETA does not itself grant work permission. A Standard Visitor visa allows only specified permitted activities and does not normally allow taking employment in the UK.

Where should I apply for a UK visitor visa?

Start with the official GOV.UK Standard Visitor visa page and follow the linked application process. Avoid unofficial websites that imitate government services.
