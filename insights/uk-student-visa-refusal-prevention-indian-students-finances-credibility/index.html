UK Student Visa: How Indian Students Can Avoid Financial and Credibility Problems

A practical, evidence-based guide to UK Student visa financial proof, genuine-student assessment, credibility interviews and compliance after arrival.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 13 min readUpdated: 25 November 2026Reviewed: 25 November 2026

An admission offer and Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) do not, by themselves, guarantee a UK Student visa or a smooth arrival. UKVI assesses whether the applicant meets the Immigration Rules and, where relevant, whether the applicant is a genuine student. Separately, students must continue to comply with enrolment, attendance, fee and visa conditions after reaching the UK.

Why this matters: UK Home Office caseworker guidance identifies education history, course relevance, future plans, immigration history and credible finances as factors in genuine-student assessment. Indian parliamentary material has also recorded cases of Indian students facing deportation or denied entry abroad after breaches such as unpaid fees, insufficient funds, attendance problems, unauthorised work and incomplete enrolment procedures.

1. Before applying: make the course choice defensible

UKVI may consider why you chose the UK, why you selected the particular university and course, how the course relates to your previous education or work, and what you plan to do afterwards. This is not a demand for a rehearsed script. It is a test of whether your actual decision makes sense and whether your answers are consistent with the documents.

  • Read the actual course structure, modules, assessment method and duration—not only the university marketing page.
  • Be able to explain why the course is appropriate for your academic background and career direction.
  • Compare relevant alternatives and explain the specific academic or professional reason for your choice.
  • Understand the university's location, accommodation plan, fees and realistic living costs.
  • Disclose previous visa refusals and immigration history accurately wherever asked.

2. Financial evidence: amount, source and timing all matter

UK Student visa financial evidence is not simply about showing a large balance on the day of application. GOV.UK requires the money to be held for the required 28 consecutive days, with evidence dated within 31 days before the visa application. The amount must cover outstanding course fees shown on the CAS plus the applicable living-cost requirement. Current amounts and exemptions must be checked on the official page at the time of application.

Permitted routes include the applicant's own money, qualifying money held by a parent or partner (with the required relationship/consent evidence), an eligible student loan, or official financial sponsorship. A bank balance from an unrelated relative, overdraft, cryptocurrency, shares or an informal promise of support should not be assumed to qualify. If using a loan, check that the lender and letter meet the specific rules and that funds will be available in time.

Practical financial-document check

  • Calculate the unpaid tuition from the CAS and add the required living-cost amount.
  • Check the correct monthly living-cost figure for the course location and the maximum period required under the current rules.
  • Confirm the account holder is an accepted person and the account is regulated and accessible.
  • Check the 28-day holding period and the 31-day document freshness rule.
  • Use the official conversion method for foreign-currency balances and retain the bank's original statements or acceptable evidence.
  • Make sure any large deposits have a genuine, documented source and do not rely on a temporary unexplained transfer.

3. Credibility interview: answer from your own research

Some applicants may be invited to a credibility interview. Home Office guidance says the assessment can consider education and immigration history, course knowledge, reasons for choosing the UK and institution, future plans, personal and financial circumstances, and English ability where relevant. No single answer guarantees approval.

Prepare by reviewing your own application, not by memorising an agent's sample answers. Be ready to explain:

  • Why this course, at this institution, at this stage of your education or career.
  • What subjects or modules you will study and how they connect to your previous experience.
  • How you selected the university and what alternatives you considered.
  • How tuition and living costs will be paid, who is funding you and where the funds came from.
  • Where you intend to live, how you will travel to campus and how you will manage your budget.
  • What you intend to do after the course, without making claims that conflict with the visa rules.

4. After arrival: admission is not the finish line

The Government of India has recorded examples of students facing enforcement action abroad for failure to maintain required bank balances, failure to pay university fees, insufficient class attendance, unauthorised employment or business activity, incomplete admission documents, failure to complete enrolment, and withdrawal from the registered programme. These examples are not a claim that every student is at risk; they show why compliance must continue after travel.

  • Complete in-person or online enrolment steps by the university's deadline.
  • Attend classes and engage with the course as required by the institution.
  • Pay tuition instalments on time or communicate with the university before a payment problem becomes a breach.
  • Follow the work conditions printed on your immigration permission; do not assume self-employment or freelance work is permitted.
  • Keep your contact details and address updated as required.
  • Speak to the university's international student compliance team before changing course, taking a break, withdrawing or transferring.

5. Warning signs that deserve attention

  • An adviser tells you to memorise answers without understanding the course.
  • Someone proposes fabricated bank statements, employment letters, experience certificates or academic records.
  • You are told to borrow money temporarily just to display a balance without checking the genuine source and holding-period rules.
  • You do not know the total tuition, deposit, refund policy or realistic monthly budget.
  • You plan to work beyond your permitted hours or rely on unauthorised work to finance essential costs.
  • You intend to stop attending or change your course without first checking the immigration consequences.

6. If a visa is refused

Read the refusal notice carefully and identify the exact rule and evidence issue. Do not immediately submit the same application with a generic cover letter. Check whether the refusal identifies missing financial evidence, a document-verification concern, a credibility issue or another requirement. Obtain qualified advice where the legal route or review rights are unclear. Never conceal a previous refusal in a later application.

Current UK Student visa financial requirement: figures to calculate

For applications under the current published rules, an applicant generally needs to show the outstanding course fees for one academic year (up to nine months) as stated on the CAS, plus living costs based on the study location. The current monthly figures are:

London£1,529£13,761
Outside London£1,171£10,539

These are maintenance figures, not the full cost of studying. Add the outstanding course fees shown on the CAS. If you have already paid eligible tuition or university-arranged accommodation deposits, check whether and how the payment can be reflected or offset under the rules. Keep the university receipt and ensure the CAS records the payment where required.

Official references: GOV.UK — Student visa: money you need and UKVI — financial evidence guidance.

The 28-day rule: how to avoid a timing mistake

Where you must provide bank evidence under the standard financial rules, the required money generally needs to be held for at least 28 consecutive days. The end date of that period must fall within 31 days before the date you submit the visa application. The closing balance must still meet the required amount after any currency conversion and relevant deductions.

  • Use statements or other evidence types expressly permitted by UKVI; make sure the account is with a regulated institution and the funds are immediately accessible.
  • If using a parent's account, provide the required parental relationship evidence and written consent for the funds to be used for your studies.
  • If using a student loan, check the letter's date, lender eligibility, amount, student identity and confirmation that funds will be available before the course begins.
  • If relying on official financial sponsorship, check that the sponsor and the letter meet the rules and that the CAS records sponsorship where required.
  • Do not assume a fixed deposit, investment, overdraft, cryptocurrency or a friend's bank balance qualifies. Follow the specific permitted evidence categories.

UKVI can verify documents with the issuing bank or sponsor. A document that cannot be verified, a sudden unexplained deposit or a mismatch between the source and the claimed funds can create serious problems.

Prepare for a credibility interview without memorising answers

UKVI may ask about your education history, course content, choice of provider, finances, future plans and immigration history. Prepare by knowing your own application—not by memorising an agent-written script.

  • Explain two or three specific course modules and how they relate to your background.
  • Be able to explain why this provider and course fit better than realistic alternatives you considered.
  • Know who is paying, how much has been paid, what remains due and how living costs will be met.
  • Explain any study gap, change of subject or previous refusal honestly and consistently with your form.
  • If you do not know an answer, do not invent facts. Ask for clarification or explain what you genuinely know.

If UKVI refuses the application

Read the refusal notice carefully and distinguish a missing or defective document from a substantive finding about credibility, finances, deception or eligibility. Check whether the decision gives a right to administrative review and the deadline, or whether a new application is the appropriate route. A fresh application should address the precise reason with verifiable evidence; simply repeating the same documents is unlikely to resolve it.

Never conceal a previous refusal, alter financial documents or submit a scripted explanation that is not true. If the refusal alleges deception, false documents or a complex breach of the Immigration Rules, get advice from an appropriately regulated UK immigration adviser or solicitor before submitting another application.

After arrival: protect your Student permission

  • Complete enrolment and registration by the university's deadline.
  • Understand attendance, engagement and reporting obligations for your course.
  • Follow the work conditions printed on or linked to your immigration permission; do not assume self-employment or freelance work is allowed.
  • Contact the university's international student compliance team before changing course, institution, study mode or taking an authorised break.
  • Keep your passport and eVisa details current and check your digital immigration status before travel.

Frequently asked questions

Does a university offer or CAS guarantee a UK Student visa?

No. UKVI makes the immigration decision separately and assesses whether the applicant meets the Student route requirements.

Can I use my uncle's or friend's bank account?

Do not assume so. GOV.UK specifies which account holders and financial routes are acceptable. Check the current rules before relying on third-party funds.

Is the 28-day rule still relevant?

Yes, where the applicant must provide financial evidence under the standard rules. The money generally needs to be held for 28 consecutive days and the evidence must meet the 31-day timing rule. Verify the current guidance and any applicable exception.

Can I work while studying?

Work conditions depend on the immigration permission and course. Follow the exact conditions attached to your permission and university guidance; do not assume self-employment is allowed.

What if I need to pause or change my course?

Contact the university's international student compliance team before acting. A change, withdrawal or interruption can affect sponsorship and immigration permission.
