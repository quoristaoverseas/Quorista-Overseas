NAWA Scholarships in Poland: A Guide for Indian Students

Poland's National Agency for Academic Exchange administers several programmes for international students. Learn which NAWA opportunities may be relevant to Indian applicants—and why programme-specific eligibility matters.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 10 min readUpdated: 25 November 2026

Poland has become an increasingly considered destination for international education, and the Polish National Agency for Academic Exchange—known as NAWA—administers scholarship and academic exchange programmes for foreign students, researchers and institutions. For Indian students, one programme worth understanding is the Stefan Banach NAWA Scholarship Programme, which supports eligible applicants from selected developing countries pursuing second-cycle (master's-level) studies in Poland.

NAWA is not a single scholarship with one universal eligibility list. Different programmes have different target countries, academic levels, fields, language requirements and funding arrangements. An Indian applicant should therefore begin with the programme's official call, not a general claim that “NAWA is open to everyone”.

1. What is NAWA?

NAWA is Poland's national agency for academic exchange. It administers programmes that support international students and researchers, strengthen Polish higher education and develop international academic cooperation. Its scholarship portfolio includes programmes for foreign students, people of Polish origin, selected nationalities and academic mobility.

The official Polish study portal also lists NAWA alongside other scholarship and mobility opportunities. The existence of a programme does not mean every nationality or degree level is eligible in each recruitment round.

2. Banach NAWA: an important option to investigate

The Stefan Banach NAWA Scholarship Programme is designed to support citizens of selected developing countries who plan to pursue full-time second-cycle studies at eligible Polish higher education institutions. The programme may offer study in Polish or English, subject to the call and the selected institution's offer.

NAWA's published programme information describes benefits that can include a monthly scholarship, exemption from tuition fees at public universities, a travel allowance and language-related support. The exact amounts and eligibility list are set by the annual call. The programme page has listed India among eligible countries in a recent call, but applicants must verify the nationality list and all other conditions in the current recruitment documents.

Some editions may include a preparatory course for the Polish-language path and language/culture support for the English-language path. Do not assume that every applicant receives identical preparatory support or that all universities and fields are available.

3. Other NAWA programmes

Poland My First Choice

This programme has supported international candidates from a specified group of countries seeking full-time second-cycle study in Poland. Its eligible nationality list is distinct from Banach NAWA and may not include Indian citizens. Applicants should never infer eligibility for one NAWA programme from eligibility for another.

Anders NAWA

The General Anders Programme is intended for young people of Polish origin and has its own requirements. It is not a general scholarship route for all international applicants.

Other academic exchange and research programmes

NAWA also administers opportunities for researchers, academic staff, institutional partnerships and mobility. These are not necessarily degree scholarships. Read the programme purpose to confirm whether it funds a full degree, research stay, exchange or another activity.

4. What funding may be included?

Under Banach NAWA, the published benefit structure has included a monthly scholarship, tuition-fee exemption at public universities and a travel allowance. The Polish-language route may include a funded preparatory course, while the English-language route may include Polish language and culture classes. Benefit amounts can change by call and should be confirmed from the current programme regulations.

Even where tuition and a stipend are provided, students should budget for costs that may not be covered: visa and residence fees, document translations, medical insurance if not included, housing deposits, initial accommodation, travel beyond the stated allowance, personal expenses and any university charges outside the tuition exemption. Check whether the allowance is paid monthly, when payments begin and whether accommodation is provided separately.

5. Eligibility for Indian applicants

For Banach NAWA, applicants must meet the nationality list and other formal conditions in the relevant call. Requirements can include holding or expecting a first-cycle degree, the date by which the diploma must be obtained, not holding Polish citizenship, and meeting language and academic requirements. The programme is for second-cycle studies, so it is not a general undergraduate scholarship.

Applicants should check whether their bachelor's qualification is accepted for entry to a Polish master's programme. University admission requirements remain separate: a scholarship application does not automatically mean the university has admitted the candidate. The chosen programme may require a particular subject background, minimum grades, language proof or additional documents.

For English-taught programmes, confirm the exact accepted evidence of English proficiency with both the university and scholarship call. A medium-of-instruction letter may be accepted in some cases and rejected in others.

6. How to apply

  1. Open the official Banach NAWA programme page and download the current call, regulations and eligible-country list.
  2. Confirm that Indian citizenship and your intended study level are eligible for that edition.
  3. Check the programme's eligible fields and identify Polish universities offering a suitable full-time second-cycle degree.
  4. Review university admission requirements and apply to the university separately if required.
  5. Prepare the scholarship application, academic documents, language evidence and required declarations.
  6. Submit through NAWA's official ICT system before the stated deadline.
  7. Monitor official communications and complete university admission and visa steps if selected.

NAWA's published programme information makes clear that the agency does not act as an intermediary in university admission for some programmes. Candidates must independently participate in the institution's admission process and satisfy its requirements.

7. Documents and practical preparation

Depending on the call, applicants may need a passport, diploma or expected-graduation certificate, transcript, proof of language proficiency, CV, motivation statement, programme information, declarations and other supporting records. The university may ask for additional documents such as course descriptions, grading-scale information or certified translations.

Prepare the scholarship and university checklists separately. Confirm whether documents can be submitted as scans initially and when originals or certified copies are required. Check the validity period for language certificates and any deadline for obtaining the final bachelor's diploma.

8. Common mistakes

  • Assuming all NAWA programmes accept Indian citizens.
  • Confusing Banach NAWA with Poland My First Choice or Anders NAWA.
  • Assuming a scholarship decision replaces university admission.
  • Using old stipend amounts or previous call deadlines.
  • Ignoring the eligible-country list, degree level or diploma date rules.
  • Assuming the stipend covers every living and arrival expense.
  • Relying on an unofficial agent rather than NAWA's current regulations.

Frequently asked questions

Can Indian students apply for Banach NAWA?

India has appeared on the eligible-country list in a recent Banach NAWA call. Applicants must verify that India is included in the current call and meet all other conditions.

Does Banach NAWA fund bachelor's degrees?

No. The programme is designed for full-time second-cycle studies, which are master's-level programmes in Poland.

Does NAWA provide a monthly stipend?

Banach NAWA's published benefits include a monthly scholarship, but the amount and terms are set by the relevant call and may change.

Does NAWA arrange university admission?

Applicants generally need to apply to the chosen university separately and meet its admission requirements. Follow the programme and institution instructions.

Are all NAWA scholarships fully funded?

No. Funding differs by programme. Check tuition, stipend, travel, accommodation and other costs separately.
