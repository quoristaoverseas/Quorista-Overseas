Study in South Korea Without TOPIK: English-Taught Programmes

Explore how international students may study in South Korea without TOPIK through English-taught programmes, and why university-specific language requirements matter.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 14 min readPublished: 25 November 2026Reviewed: 25 November 2026

International students may be able to study in South Korea without a TOPIK score when they apply to a programme that accepts English proficiency evidence instead of Korean proficiency. However, this is not a blanket exemption across Korean universities. The university or department sets the language rules for each programme, and the requirements can differ for admission, graduation and scholarships.

The Korean government's Study in Korea guidance on language proficiency states that international students must meet the Korean or English proficiency requirements set by each university or department. Applicants should therefore verify the current admissions guide for their exact course and intake.

Key point: “No TOPIK required for admission” does not necessarily mean “no Korean-language study during the degree”, “no TOPIK at graduation” or “no separate scholarship conditions”. Verify each separately.

Can you study in South Korea without TOPIK?

Yes, some English-taught undergraduate and graduate programmes admit international students without TOPIK. In such cases, the applicant may need IELTS, TOEFL or another English qualification accepted by the university. Some institutions may allow an English-medium education waiver or an internal assessment, but these alternatives must be expressly confirmed.

For a Korean-taught programme, TOPIK or another accepted proof of Korean ability is more likely to be required. The precise level, accepted alternatives and timing of the requirement are institution- and department-specific.

What does “English-taught programme” really mean?

Do not rely on a brochure label alone. A university may use “English track” to describe a programme with a substantial number of English courses, even if some required modules, electives, administrative activities or practical placements use Korean.

Before applying, find out:

  • Whether all required core modules are delivered in English every semester.
  • Whether enough English-taught electives are available for your cohort to graduate on time.
  • Whether laboratory work, internships, clinical placements or fieldwork require Korean.
  • Whether course availability depends on minimum enrolment or professor availability.
  • Whether thesis supervision and defence can be completed in English.
  • Whether the university has a Korean-language graduation condition even for international students in the English track.

Ask for the curriculum or course catalogue for the actual intake year. A programme's general marketing page may not show which courses will run in English in a particular semester.

What can replace TOPIK for an English-taught degree?

Possible evidence includes:

  • IELTS or TOEFL: where the university publishes a minimum score and accepts the relevant test version.
  • Other English tests: only if listed by the university or confirmed by the department.
  • Medium of Instruction certificate: only where the admissions guide permits an English-medium waiver and the applicant meets its conditions.
  • University interview or assessment: where the institution formally uses it to assess English proficiency.

Check whether the university requires an overall score, minimum section scores, a test taken within a specified validity period or direct electronic score reporting. A waiver for admission should not be assumed to apply to a scholarship or another authority.

Will TOPIK be required to graduate?

That depends on the university and programme. Some degree tracks may impose Korean-language credits, a TOPIK level or another proficiency condition before graduation; others may not. The government's Study in Korea portal explicitly distinguishes university-set admission and graduation requirements, so applicants should not stop checking after receiving an offer.

Ask the department to confirm the graduation rule in writing. If TOPIK is required later, establish the level, deadline, number of attempts and whether the university provides language courses. A student who enters without TOPIK should plan for the possibility of learning Korean for daily life, internships and employment even when the degree is taught in English.

Scholarships and the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS)

Scholarship requirements are a separate check. The Study in Korea portal and the official Global Korea Scholarship (GKS) website publish scholarship information and programme notices. Each current GKS application guideline should be read carefully for language-related scoring, preferred qualifications, Korean-language training and any conditions applicable to the selected track.

Do not assume that an English-taught degree automatically removes every Korean-language condition attached to a scholarship. Conversely, do not assume that every GKS applicant must meet the same TOPIK condition at the point of application. The current annual guideline and the relevant university/track instructions are decisive.

D-2 degree study and D-4-1 Korean language study are different

For general orientation, the Korean government study portal identifies D-2 subcategories for degree study (including master's study) and D-4-1 for Korean language training. These are different study purposes and should not be treated as interchangeable.

An applicant admitted to an English-taught master's programme should follow the current visa instructions for the relevant degree-study category. A student enrolling in a Korean language institute should check the requirements for the language-training route. Admission to a programme does not itself guarantee visa issuance; immigration authorities and the Korean diplomatic mission assess the visa application under current rules.

Check the latest instructions from Hi Korea and the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in India. Do not rely on old blog posts or assume that language-test rules for admission are identical to visa-document requirements.

Checklist: verify a Korean programme before paying an application fee

  • Correct university, department and degree title
  • Current international admissions guide and intake
  • Official language of instruction for core modules
  • Accepted English tests and minimum scores
  • Written confirmation of any MOI waiver
  • TOPIK admission requirement, if any
  • TOPIK or Korean credits required for graduation
  • English course availability each semester
  • Scholarship-specific language conditions
  • Current visa category and document checklist
  1. Download the current intake guide from the university's official website.
  2. Read the general international admission rules and the department's programme-specific rules.
  3. Check the curriculum and course catalogue rather than relying only on the “English track” label.
  4. Email the international admissions office and, if necessary, the department coordinator.
  5. Ask for written clarification on both admission and graduation language conditions.
  6. Verify scholarship and visa requirements independently before paying deposits.

Confirmed Korean university examples

South Korea does not have one universal TOPIK rule for every graduate programme. Some departments use English proficiency instead of TOPIK; others require Korean proficiency even when a university has English-taught courses. The department's current intake guide is decisive.

POSTECH graduate admissions: Korean is not essential for application

POSTECH's international graduate admissions FAQ says Korean proficiency is not an essential application requirement, although applicants with some Korean ability may be preferred. It also confirms that an MOI certificate can be submitted instead of an English test where the applicant meets the applicable exemption requirements. The document must confirm that the relevant degree programme was conducted entirely in English and be officially issued/certified by the university. This is a specific published rule, not an exemption that applies to every Korean university.

Ajou University: language rules vary by department

Ajou University's graduate admissions page states that each department and major has different language requirements. Its published table includes routes using TOPIK or specified English scores for some categories, while another category permits TOPIK Level 4 only. Applicants must therefore check the exact department and the relevant admission guideline rather than relying on the university-wide summary.

Before applying: confirm the programme's language of instruction for the actual intake, whether all required courses are offered in English, the accepted English tests or MOI wording, any TOPIK condition before graduation, and any scholarship-specific language rule. Admission without TOPIK does not mean Korean will never be required during study or for later employment.

Confirmed university routes: what the published rules actually say

These examples show why applicants must read both university-wide requirements and the exact department guide. An English-language route at admission does not automatically remove Korean-language study or graduation requirements.

POSTECH graduate admissions: an MOI route is possible in qualifying cases

POSTECH's international graduate admissions FAQ says Korean proficiency is not essential for application, although some Korean ability may be advantageous. It also confirms that an MOI document may replace an English test where the applicant meets its exemption conditions. The document must confirm that the entire degree programme was taught in English and be officially issued and certified by the appropriate authority at the previous university. POSTECH describes alternative sealed institutional documents with prescribed wording if a separate MOI letter is unavailable. Applicants must still meet the current admission guide and programme requirements.

Ajou University Graduate School: some categories accept English scores; others require TOPIK

Ajou states that language requirements differ by department and major. Its published general table includes a category accepting TOPIK Level 4 or specified English scores, including TOEFL iBT 71 or IELTS 5.5, while another category is restricted to TOPIK Level 4. An English score is therefore not an alternative for every Ajou department. Identify the department's category in the current intake guide before applying.

Ajou International & IT Business: an English track can still require Korean study

The department's published graduation information distinguishes Korean and English tracks. Even the English track includes 18 credits of designated Korean-language courses and publishes language-certificate conditions for graduation. This is a concrete reason to inspect curriculum and graduation regulations, not just the “English track” label.

Admission without TOPIK does not guarantee graduation without Korean

Ask the department to answer these separately: (1) Is TOPIK required when applying? (2) If not, which English tests or MOI documents are accepted? (3) Are Korean-language credits compulsory during the degree? (4) Is a TOPIK level or other language certificate required before graduation? (5) Are there exceptions for international students or the specific English track? (6) Are internships, clinical placements, research activities and thesis supervision available in English?

Important: An English-track student may still need Korean for daily life, part-time work, internships and post-study employment. That is different from a formal academic graduation requirement, but it should influence preparation and budgeting.

How to assess whether an English-taught programme is genuinely suitable

  1. Check the actual curriculum: count required core modules offered in English in each semester, not just the number of English courses in a brochure.
  2. Check availability for your cohort: ask whether English modules run every year and whether a small cohort can affect course availability.
  3. Check thesis and supervisor language: confirm research supervision, lab instruction, thesis submission and defence can be completed in English where relevant.
  4. Check compulsory Korean: obtain the graduation regulation and ask how many Korean credits or what TOPIK level is required.
  5. Check scholarship conditions: funding may have separate TOPIK, GPA, attendance or renewal requirements.
  6. Check career implications: assess the Korean-language level expected in the occupation you intend to pursue. Admission eligibility and employability are different questions.

Do not confuse a degree programme with a Korean-language course

A degree programme and a Korean-language training programme are different study purposes and generally use different immigration categories. Degree-seeking study normally involves the relevant D-2 category, while a qualifying university-affiliated Korean-language course may involve D-4-1. The correct category depends on the actual institution and course. Admission does not guarantee visa issuance or a particular visa route. Check current instructions from Hi Korea and the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in India.
