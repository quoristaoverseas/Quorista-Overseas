Study in South Korea Without TOPIK: English-Taught Programmes

Explore how international students may study in South Korea without TOPIK through English-taught programmes, and why university-specific language requirements matter.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 10 min readPublished: 25 November 2026

TOPIK is commonly used for Korean-taught degree programmes, but it is not universally required for every international student or every degree in South Korea. Universities and departments set their own language requirements.

Students seeking English-taught programmes may instead need evidence of English proficiency, such as IELTS, TOEFL or another accepted measure. Requirements vary and a course described as “English track” may still have Korean-language requirements.

Is TOPIK mandatory for all South Korean universities?

No. The Korean government's Study in Korea guidance explains that language requirements are set by universities or departments. Korean-taught programmes commonly require Korean proficiency, while English-taught programmes may require English proficiency instead. Specific rules and exemptions differ.

What are English-taught programmes?

These are programmes or courses where instruction is delivered in English. The label can be used differently across institutions: a programme may be fully taught in English, offer only selected English courses or mix English and Korean instruction. Check curriculum, course availability and graduation requirements.

What might be required instead of TOPIK?

  • IELTS, TOEFL or another English test accepted by the programme
  • English-medium education documentation, where a university accepts it
  • Department-specific assessment or interview
  • Other language evidence expressly listed in the admissions guide

Do not assume a Medium of Instruction letter is accepted. Some universities require test scores even for applicants whose previous education was in English.

How to verify a programme's language rules

  1. Find the programme-specific international admissions guide for your intended intake.
  2. Confirm language of instruction and whether all courses are offered in English.
  3. Check required English test scores and waiver rules.
  4. Check TOPIK requirements for admission, graduation and scholarship eligibility separately.
  5. Ask the department for written clarification if requirements are unclear.
  6. Check current visa procedures and required documents separately.

Admission and visa are separate steps

Meeting a university's language requirement does not itself guarantee a visa. Visa eligibility, financial evidence, documentation and procedures depend on the visa category and current official instructions.

FAQs

Can I study in South Korea without TOPIK?

Yes, some programmes do not require TOPIK, especially where instruction and admission are in English. Confirm the specific programme policy.

Do English-taught master's programmes require IELTS?

Many require English proficiency evidence, but accepted tests and waiver conditions vary.

Will I need TOPIK to graduate?

Some programmes have Korean-language graduation requirements; others may not. Check the university rules.

Can I apply with an MOI certificate?

Only if the programme explicitly accepts it or confirms a waiver.

Official source

Study in Korea (government portal) — Language Proficiency Requirements & TOPIK. Consult the university's current international admissions guide and department.

Editorial note: General information only. Requirements vary by institution, department, intake and visa category. Verify current rules. No admission or visa outcome is guaranteed.
