Study in Poland Without IELTS: English-Language Alternatives for Indian Students

A practical guide to English-language admission in Poland for Indian students, including accepted B2 certificates, PTE, TOEFL, MOI evidence, current rules and visa-related cautions.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 12 min readPublished: 25 November 2026

Indian students researching Poland may find universities that do not require IELTS specifically. That does not mean English-language proficiency can be ignored. Poland's current admissions framework requires proof of the language of instruction at the applicable level, and universities publish the certificates or documents they accept.

Practical answer: You may be able to apply without IELTS if the university accepts another recognised B2 certificate or a qualifying English-medium degree document. Always check the exact programme and the visa-document rules that apply to your case. A university's admission decision and the consulate's assessment are separate stages.

1. What changed in Poland's language-evidence rules?

Poland's official guidance describes a B2 language-evidence requirement for specified groups of non-EU applicants whose admission is based on foreign education documents. It is not a blanket statement covering every applicant and every route: the applicable rule depends on study level, the recognition status of the prior qualification and the relevant statutory category. Universities publish their own accepted-document instructions and may impose higher or more specific programme requirements.

Since the 2025 regulatory changes, applicants should check the national list of accepted B2 evidence as well as the university's current instructions. The regulation includes specified four-skill certificates (including IELTS, TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic and named Cambridge qualifications) and, in defined circumstances, a completed first-cycle, second-cycle or uniform master's diploma that identifies the language of instruction; if the diploma does not state it, the diploma supplement or a separate certificate must do so. An informal MOI letter for an unfinished degree, or a test accepted under an older policy, should not be assumed to qualify. Check the statutory regulation and the university's current non-EU admissions page together.

2. What can replace IELTS?

IELTS is one way to demonstrate English proficiency. Depending on the university's published list and the current rules, accepted alternatives may include:

  • PTE Academic: accepted by some universities at a specified B2-equivalent score.
  • TOEFL iBT: accepted by institutions that list it, subject to the required version, score and verification method.
  • Cambridge English qualifications: such as B2 First, C1 Advanced or C2 Proficiency where the university lists them.
  • Other recognised certificates: for example LanguageCert, Trinity, telc or Pearson English International Certificate, but only where the institution's current list includes the exact qualification.
  • English-medium degree evidence: some institutions accept a prior bachelor's or master's degree taught entirely in English when the official transcript or diploma supplement clearly confirms English as the sole medium of instruction.

Do not assume that Duolingo English Test is accepted everywhere. A university may list it for one applicant category or under an older policy while the current non-EU rules or visa stage require different evidence.

3. A confirmed example: University of Wrocław

The University of Wrocław publishes a specific B2 English-evidence list for candidates from outside the EU. It lists IELTS Academic or General at 5.5, PTE Academic at 59, TOEFL iBT (with score rules that changed for tests taken from 21 January 2026), Cambridge qualifications, Trinity, LanguageCert and other certificates. It also lists a diploma of completed higher education where English was the only medium of instruction, provided the transcript, diploma or supplement confirms that fact.

This is a concrete example of an IELTS alternative and a conditional MOI route. The university says it requires all four language skills to be tested for the certificates where components can be selected, and it states that certificates must not be older than three years. Programme-specific requirements may differ.

Important caution about Duolingo and MOI

The University of Wrocław's page separates requirements for non-EU and EU candidates. Duolingo appears in the EU-candidate list, not the non-EU list. Its admissions update also warns that language evidence must meet the current B2 and documentary conditions. Indian applicants should therefore not rely on Duolingo unless the university confirms in writing that the exact current intake accepts it for their applicant category.

For MOI, the university's update explains that English-medium education should be stated clearly in the transcript or diploma supplement, or otherwise satisfy its published documentary criteria. A generic letter that simply says “medium of instruction: English” may not be sufficient if it does not meet the institution's exact conditions.

4. Can an Indian MOI certificate replace IELTS?

Sometimes, but do not treat it as a universal exemption. The institution may require proof that the complete prior degree was taught entirely in English, and may insist that this is recorded in the official transcript, diploma supplement or another specified document. A letter from a college department, an individual professor or an agent's assurance is not equivalent to a published waiver policy.

Before applying, ask the university whether it accepts the exact document issued by your Indian university, whether it must cover the whole degree, whether a prescribed wording is required, and whether the exemption applies to your chosen programme and intake. Also verify whether the document meets the current B2 evidence rules applicable to the visa process.

5. Admission without IELTS does not guarantee a student visa

Polish universities assess admission under their own programme and institutional rules. The visa stage is separate from university admission. For applicants in India, the current Polish mission/VFS study-visa checklist lists IELTS (minimum 6.5), TOEFL iBT (79) and CAE (176) as highly recommended supporting evidence of language ability; it does not describe one of these tests as a universal compulsory document for every applicant. The checklist also states that the consul may assess whether the applicant's language knowledge is sufficient and may request additional documents or an interview. Do not confuse the statutory B2 document rules used in university admission with the visa-stage assessment.

For Indian applicants, confirm the latest checklist and appointment instructions with the Polish mission responsible for your place of residence. The Embassy in New Delhi and Consulate General in Mumbai have separate consular jurisdictions; use the checklist applicable to your place of residence. Do not rely on a university's old blog post or a third-party agent's statement that “IELTS is not needed for Poland”.

6. Documents to prepare

  • Passport and application form
  • Class 10/12 or degree certificates as relevant
  • Academic transcripts and diploma supplement
  • NAWA recognition or required confirmation where applicable
  • IELTS, PTE, TOEFL or other accepted B2 evidence
  • MOI document meeting the university's exact rule, if used
  • Proof that all four language skills were assessed, where required
  • Tuition payment and admission confirmation
  • Accommodation and insurance evidence
  • Financial means for the visa process
  • Translations, apostille/legalisation if required
  • Programme-specific prerequisites or entrance exam evidence

7. How to shortlist Polish universities without IELTS

  1. Choose the exact degree level, subject and intake; do not rely on a general country claim.
  2. Open the official programme page and note the minimum English level and accepted tests.
  3. Check whether the page is written for non-EU applicants and whether the current intake has updated rules.
  4. If using PTE or TOEFL, confirm the exact test version, score, validity and online verification procedure.
  5. If using MOI, obtain the official transcript/diploma supplement and ask whether it meets the university's published wording.
  6. Check the visa-stage language evidence and document-recognition requirements separately.
  7. Save the official page and written admissions confirmation before paying a non-refundable fee.

8. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Confusing “IELTS not required” with “no English proof required”.
  • Assuming a university's EU-candidate list also applies to Indian non-EU applicants.
  • Relying on Duolingo or an MOI letter without confirming the current non-EU rule.
  • Using an expired test result or a test that does not assess all required skills.
  • Assuming admission means the visa authority must accept the same evidence.
  • Ignoring NAWA recognition, legalisation, tuition payment, insurance and financial documents.

Frequently asked questions

Can Indian students study in Poland without IELTS?

Potentially, yes. Some universities accept other B2 certificates or qualifying English-medium degree evidence. The exact route depends on the institution, programme, applicant category and current visa requirements.

Does the University of Wrocław accept PTE Academic?

Its published requirements for non-EU candidates list PTE Academic with a minimum score of 59, subject to the current programme rules and validity conditions.

Does the University of Wrocław accept Duolingo for Indian applicants?

Duolingo appears in a separate EU-candidate list, not the published non-EU list. Indian applicants should not rely on it without written confirmation for their exact intake and applicant category.

Can an MOI certificate from an Indian university be used?

It may be accepted by some institutions if it meets their exact conditions. At the University of Wrocław, the official transcript, diploma or supplement must confirm that the higher-education programme was conducted only in English. Confirm whether your document meets the current rules.

Is B2 English proof required for Poland?

The official Study in Poland guidance describes a B2 language requirement for the relevant foreign applicants and study categories. The exact evidence depends on the applicant's prior education and the programme.

Does admission without IELTS guarantee a Polish student visa?

No. Admission and visa decisions are separate. The consulate may require evidence and documents under the current rules, even if a university has accepted an alternative route.
