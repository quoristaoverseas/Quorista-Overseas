Thailand Travel for Indians in 2026: 30-Day Visa Exemption, TDAC and Entry Checklist

A practical guide to the current Thailand entry rules for Indian ordinary passport holders, including the revised 30-day visa exemption and the free digital arrival card.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 12 min readUpdated: 25 November 2026

Thailand remains a popular short-break destination for Indian travellers, but older articles and social-media posts may still mention the earlier 60-day visa-exemption arrangement. The Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi published a revised arrangement that took effect on 15 September 2026: Indian ordinary passport holders entering for tourism may use a 30-day visa exemption, subject to the applicable entry conditions and immigration decision.

Important update — checked 25 November 2026: Do not plan a 60-day stay on the basis of older posts. The Thai Embassy's September 2026 announcement states that the revised 30-day exemption for Indian ordinary passport holders is effective from 15 September 2026. Check the Embassy's latest notice again shortly before departure.

1. What is the current Thailand visa rule for Indian tourists?

Under the revised arrangement, Indian ordinary passport holders travelling to Thailand for tourism can enter without obtaining a tourist visa in advance for a permitted stay of up to 30 days. This is a visa exemption, not a visa-on-arrival sticker and not a general permission to work, study or take up residence.

The 30-day period is the current rule identified in the Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi's notice dated 15 September 2026. The earlier temporary 60-day arrangement should not be used to calculate a new trip after the revised rule took effect. The exact permission granted at the border is recorded by Thai immigration; travellers should check the entry stamp or electronic record and leave within the authorised period.

A visa exemption also does not guarantee admission. Border officials may ask questions or request evidence about the purpose, duration, accommodation and means of support for the visit. Carry genuine documents that match the trip you are actually taking.

2. Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC): mandatory before entry

Thailand's Digital Arrival Card replaces the traditional paper arrival card. The official Thai Immigration Bureau system says that all non-Thai nationals entering Thailand must submit the TDAC before arrival, regardless of whether they are travelling visa-exempt, with a visa or using visa on arrival.

  • When: Submit the details within the three days before the arrival date (the official system describes this as up to 72 hours before entry).
  • Where: Use only tdac.immigration.go.th.
  • Fee: The official TDAC portal states that the arrival card is free. Be cautious of unofficial websites charging a service fee or presenting themselves as the government portal.
  • Information: Keep passport details, flight and arrival information, address in Thailand, travel purpose and the requested health-declaration information ready.
  • Each trip: The official FAQ says the card is single-use for the expected arrival. Complete a new submission for each new entry.

Save the confirmation or submitted card where you can access it offline. Check every field carefully against the passport and current itinerary. If a flight, accommodation or other detail changes, use the official update function where available or submit corrected information before entry.

TDAC is not a visa. Completing it does not extend a permitted stay, replace a visa where one is required, or guarantee admission. It is a separate pre-arrival information requirement.

TDAC: transit exceptions and family submissions

The Immigration Bureau's official FAQ adds two useful practical details:

  • Airside transit: a technical landing or transit where you do not pass through Thai immigration does not require a TDAC. If you leave the airport or pass immigration to enter Thailand during a connection, submit the TDAC before entry.
  • Children and infants: each non-Thai traveller, including children and infants, needs the required arrival-card submission.
  • Family/group form: the official system permits up to 10 travellers in one group submission. Check each person's passport details carefully.
  • Multiple entries: the card is tied to the expected arrival and is single-use. Submit a fresh TDAC for each new entry into Thailand.

These points are set out in the Thai Immigration Bureau's official TDAC FAQ. If your itinerary includes a self-transfer, airport change or overnight connection, confirm whether you must clear immigration; do not assume that every itinerary labelled “transit” is airside.

3. Thailand entry checklist for Indian travellers

Prepare a small, consistent set of documents. The exact questions asked at the border can vary, so there is no single document bundle that guarantees entry.

  • Valid Indian ordinary passport and any required visa or evidence that you qualify for the exemption.
  • Confirmed or onward travel details consistent with the length of the intended visit.
  • Accommodation address and booking or host details for the planned stay.
  • Evidence that you can meet the costs of the visit, in a form you can readily access.
  • A realistic itinerary and explanation of the purpose of travel.
  • Completed TDAC confirmation and access to the information submitted.
  • Travel insurance details and emergency contacts, where arranged.

Do not create fictitious hotel bookings, onward tickets or financial evidence. If plans are flexible, explain them honestly and carry the genuine reservations or arrangements you do have. Keep copies of important documents separately from your passport.

4. What if you want to stay longer than 30 days?

If your intended visit exceeds the visa-exempt period, do not assume that you can simply arrive and remain for 60 days. Review the appropriate Thai visa category and apply through the official Thai e-Visa portal where applicable. The Royal Thai Embassy's visa page lists tourist visa options and explains that Thailand's e-Visa system is used for applications in India.

Choose the route based on the real purpose of travel. Tourism, business activity, employment, education, medical treatment and long-stay residence are not interchangeable purposes. A tourist exemption should not be used as permission to work or undertake an activity that requires a different immigration status.

If you are already in Thailand and want to extend or change your permission, contact the Thai Immigration Bureau before your current authorised stay expires. Do not rely on informal advice about extensions or repeated border crossings.

5. Common mistakes that can disrupt a Thailand trip

  • Relying on old articles that still state a 60-day exemption for Indian tourists.
  • Confusing visa exemption with visa on arrival or a tourist e-Visa.
  • Paying an unofficial website to complete a TDAC that is free on the official portal.
  • Submitting the TDAC too early, using incorrect passport details or forgetting to submit it for a child.
  • Assuming a visa exemption means guaranteed entry or permission to work.
  • Booking a stay longer than the current permitted period without obtaining the correct immigration permission.
  • Ignoring the actual stay-until date recorded by immigration at entry.

Frequently asked questions

Can Indians visit Thailand without a visa in November 2026?

Indian ordinary passport holders travelling for tourism are covered by the 30-day visa-exemption arrangement effective 15 September 2026, subject to current conditions and the decision of Thai immigration.

Is Thailand still giving Indians 60 days visa-free?

Do not rely on the earlier 60-day arrangement for a new trip after 15 September 2026. The Thai Embassy's revised notice states that the Indian tourist exemption is now 30 days.

Is the Thailand Digital Arrival Card free?

Yes. The official Thai Immigration Bureau TDAC portal states that no fee is required. Use the government portal rather than paid lookalike websites.

When should I submit the TDAC?

Within the three days before arrival, including the arrival date. The official instructions describe the window as up to 72 hours before entry.

Do children need a TDAC?

Yes. The official TDAC FAQ says infants and children are also required to have a submission.

Does TDAC approval guarantee entry?

No. TDAC is an arrival-information form, not a visa or entry approval. Thai immigration retains authority to assess travellers at the border.
