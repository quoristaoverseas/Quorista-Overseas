Global Korea Scholarship (GKS): A Complete Guide for Indian Students

The Global Korea Scholarship can support international students pursuing degrees in South Korea. Here is how Indian applicants should understand the programme, its tracks, funding and annual application rules.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 12 min readUpdated: 25 November 2026

The Global Korea Scholarship (GKS) is a South Korean government scholarship programme designed to invite talented international students to study at participating Korean institutions. It is administered through the National Institute for International Education (NIIED) and is one of the best-known routes for international applicants seeking funded study in South Korea.

For Indian students, GKS can be attractive because the programme may combine tuition support, a monthly allowance, airfare and Korean-language training, depending on the scholarship category and current guidelines. But the exact benefits, quotas, eligible universities and application process must be checked in the annual notice.

Do not apply from an old checklist. GKS publishes separate annual guidelines for undergraduate and graduate recruitment. The 2027 undergraduate notice was published in September 2026; applicants should download that year's official files rather than relying on previous cycles.

1. What is the Global Korea Scholarship?

GKS is a government-invited scholarship for international students pursuing eligible degree programmes in South Korea. It aims to promote international educational exchange and develop global talent. Participating universities and departments are listed in the official annual university information files.

The programme is not a general tuition discount offered by every Korean university. It is a structured national scholarship with eligibility conditions, selection stages and a defined list of eligible institutions or departments. An applicant should confirm that the intended course appears in the applicable university information file.

2. Degree options and application tracks

GKS has undergraduate and graduate recruitment. Undergraduate opportunities may include bachelor's and associate-degree routes. Graduate recruitment generally covers master's and doctoral degrees, alongside specialised categories where announced. The duration and structure depend on the degree and programme.

Embassy Track

Under the Embassy Track, applicants submit through the Korean diplomatic mission responsible for their country of citizenship or residence, following the current instructions. The embassy conducts an initial stage and forwards selected applications through the GKS process. The number of university choices and permitted combinations can differ by year and track.

University Track

Under the University Track, applications are submitted to a participating university or designated programme. This route may include general or specialised categories. Applicants must check the institution's own deadline and submission method as well as NIIED's central guidance.

Tracks are not interchangeable. Some categories may be restricted by degree level, field, institution, nationality or other conditions. Do not submit duplicate or multiple applications unless the current guideline explicitly permits the combination.

3. What does GKS cover?

The official Study in Korea scholarship information describes benefits that can include airfare, Korean-language training fees, tuition and monthly allowances. The precise package and amounts are set out in the current GKS guidelines and may vary by scholarship category, degree level and award conditions.

Check the annual document for the amount and duration of the monthly allowance, tuition coverage limits, airfare conditions, language training exemptions or requirements, medical insurance and any settlement or completion benefits. A stipend is intended to support living costs; it should not be assumed to cover every personal expense in every Korean city.

Also check what happens if you already have the required Korean-language proficiency, if your degree is taught in English, or if your programme falls under a specialised category. Some pathways have different language-training arrangements.

4. Eligibility for Indian applicants

GKS eligibility is determined by the current annual guidelines. Common criteria include the applicant's nationality and parents' nationality, age, prior education, academic performance and health. For graduate applications, the applicant normally needs the relevant previous degree or expected graduation by the stated date. Undergraduate eligibility is based on the specified secondary-school or equivalent qualification.

Academic requirements are not simply “80% for everyone”. Official rules may describe a minimum percentage, class ranking or CGPA conversion threshold. Use the exact formula and accepted grading evidence in the relevant year. Do not convert your marks using an online calculator unless the guidelines permit that method.

Some categories have different age limits or additional requirements. Applicants should review disqualification rules too, including restrictions relating to Korean citizenship, previous GKS participation or other conditions described in the official notice.

5. How to apply

  1. Download the correct undergraduate or graduate guidelines and all annexes from the official GKS notice page.
  2. Check that your nationality, age, qualification, academic record and intended field meet the rules.
  3. Review the participating university and department information. Confirm that the programme accepts international GKS scholars at your intended degree level.
  4. Choose the correct track and follow the embassy or university submission instructions.
  5. Prepare the application forms, study plan, personal statement and supporting records in the required format.
  6. Submit by the deadline and retain proof of submission. Follow official instructions for interviews, document verification and later rounds.

For Embassy Track, use the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in India's current notice for India-specific instructions. For University Track, verify the selected institution's own application window and document delivery rules. A central GKS notice does not replace a university's detailed instructions.

6. Documents and application preparation

Depending on the year and track, applicants may need application forms, personal statement, study plan, recommendation letter, academic transcripts and certificates, proof of nationality and family relationship, passport copy, language scores, awards or publications, and medical self-assessment forms. Graduate applicants may need a research proposal or portfolio for particular fields.

Check whether documents must be notarised, apostilled, consularly legalised, translated or submitted as original hard copies. Requirements can differ between the initial application and the final nominee stage. Never assume a scan uploaded online is sufficient for every later stage.

Your study plan should show that you have researched the Korean institution and the intended subject. Explain why the programme is appropriate, how you will approach Korean-language study if required and what academic or professional goals you plan to pursue. Avoid generic statements that could be copied into an application to any country.

7. Selection stages and what makes an application competitive

The process generally involves screening through the relevant embassy or university, followed by further review under the NIIED process and final confirmation. Exact stages, interview requirements and timelines vary by track and annual call. Being nominated at an early stage does not necessarily mean the scholarship is finally awarded.

Competitive applicants usually demonstrate strong academic preparation, a clear fit with the selected programme, realistic study goals and careful attention to instructions. Language ability may strengthen an application, but the required evidence depends on the current call and programme. Do not submit inflated claims or documents that cannot be verified.

8. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using a previous year's quota, benefit amount, age rule or deadline as if it were current.
  • Selecting a university or department that is not listed for the relevant track.
  • Confusing undergraduate and graduate requirements.
  • Missing a university-specific deadline or document delivery requirement.
  • Assuming every GKS category includes the same Korean-language training period.
  • Failing to follow document authentication or translation instructions.
  • Assuming scholarship selection automatically resolves admission, immigration or visa formalities.

Frequently asked questions

Can Indian students apply for GKS?

Indian citizens may apply if they meet the nationality and all other conditions in the applicable annual guidelines and the India-specific notice.

Does GKS cover tuition and living expenses?

The programme's published benefits include tuition support and monthly allowances, alongside other possible benefits. Check the current category-specific guidelines for exact coverage and amounts.

Can I apply for any Korean university?

No. You must choose from the participating universities and departments listed for the relevant GKS track and year.

Is IELTS compulsory?

Not universally. Language evidence and its role depend on the current GKS guidelines, university and programme. Korean or English proficiency may be relevant in different ways.

Does GKS guarantee a student visa?

No scholarship award should be described as a visa guarantee. Applicants must complete the applicable immigration process and meet the relevant requirements.
