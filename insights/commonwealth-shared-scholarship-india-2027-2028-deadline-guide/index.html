Commonwealth Shared Scholarship 2027/28: Indian Applicants, Two Portals and Deadlines

A deadline-focused guide for Indian applicants covering the CSC Central and Ministry of Education SAKSHAT submissions, eligibility, approved courses, funding and application strategy.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 13 min readUpdated: 25 November 2026

The Commonwealth Shared Scholarship is a competitive UK-funded route for students from eligible Commonwealth countries who want to complete an approved, full-time taught master's programme and use the training to contribute to development in their home country. For Indian applicants, the 2027/28 cycle has an important two-portal requirement: the CSC Central application and the Government of India's SAKSHAT application are separate submissions.

Deadline alert — checked 25 November 2026: the CSC Central portal closes at 16:00 GMT on 8 December 2026. India's Ministry of Education SAKSHAT portal closes earlier, on 30 November 2026. An applicant must not assume that submitting on one portal completes the other.

1. Two applications, two different deadlines

The CSC's official 2027/28 page says applications opened on 10 November 2026 and close on 8 December 2026 at 16:00 GMT. The Ministry of Education's Indian notice separately invites Indian citizens who are permanent residents of India to apply and states that SAKSHAT is active from 30 October to 30 November 2026.

Do not treat the Ministry portal as a substitute for CSC Central, or vice versa. Read the current Ministry notice carefully for its document requirements and submission instructions. If any instruction appears inconsistent, contact the Ministry's External Scholarship Division through the contact details in the official notice rather than relying on social-media summaries.

2. Who can apply?

The CSC states that candidates must be citizens of, and permanently resident in, an eligible Commonwealth country. India is on the eligible-country list. Applicants must be available to start in September/October 2027, and by September 2027 must hold a first degree at least equivalent to an upper second-class (2:1) honours degree, or a lower second-class degree plus a relevant postgraduate qualification, usually a master's.

The scheme is intended for people who would not otherwise be able to afford UK study and who can show the potential to contribute to development in their home country. Candidates must not have studied or worked for more than one academic year in a high-income country. The detailed eligibility conditions and exceptions on the official CSC page control; this is not a general scholarship for every Indian applicant.

3. Choose an approved course before writing your application

The award is for a one-year, full-time master's course at a participating UK university, and the course must be on the CSC's approved 2027/28 list. The participating universities and approved courses are cycle-specific. Do not assume that a course eligible in a previous year remains eligible this year.

Check the current approved-course list linked from the CSC page, then open the university's own course page. Confirm academic entry requirements, English-language evidence, application deadline, course start date and whether you must apply for admission separately. The scholarship application does not itself create an offer of admission.

4. What does the scholarship cover?

The CSC describes a package that includes approved return airfare, full approved tuition fees, a monthly living allowance (currently stated as £1,712, or £2,000 for London metropolitan universities), and certain study-related allowances such as a thesis grant where applicable and a study travel grant. A contribution towards a mandatory TB test may also be available where required.

Rates and entitlements are subject to the current handbook and award conditions. Family allowances are limited and conditional; they should not be interpreted as a promise that the full cost of bringing dependants will be met. Read the official financial-assistance section and current handbook before making plans.

5. What makes the application competitive?

The CSC asks applicants to explain their study objectives, how the course will help meet them, career plans for the five years after the award and longer-term goals, and how they intend to create development impact. The form also asks for a detailed plan of study, personal statement, employment history and details of referees. The CSC lists candidate merit, quality of the study/research proposal and potential development impact among its selection criteria.

  • Course fit: connect the course modules and learning outcomes to a defined professional or development problem.
  • Evidence of impact: use specific work, community, research or leadership examples rather than broad claims about wanting to help India.
  • Career pathway: explain a realistic role or sector in which the new skills will be used after returning.
  • References: choose referees who can assess your ability and future contribution, and give them enough time to prepare informed references.
  • Consistency: ensure your CV, employment history, study plan and personal statement tell the same credible story.

6. A practical timeline for Indian applicants

  1. Immediately: check the current approved courses and university admission requirements; shortlist only courses that match your academic background and goals.
  2. Before 30 November: complete the SAKSHAT submission in line with the Ministry notice. Do not leave document uploads or portal registration until the final hours.
  3. Before 8 December, 16:00 GMT: submit the CSC Central application. Prepare longer written answers offline, check them carefully, and retain the submission reference.
  4. In parallel: apply for admission to the selected university course by its own deadline. CSC scholarship submission and university admission are distinct processes.
  5. After submission: monitor both portals and email accounts, respond to official requests, and keep copies of all submitted material.

7. Mistakes that can invalidate an otherwise strong application

  • Submitting to only one of the two required portals.
  • Choosing a course that is not on the approved 2027/28 list.
  • Assuming the scholarship application automatically applies for university admission.
  • Using generic impact statements that do not identify a real problem, intended contribution or plausible career route.
  • Leaving references, transcripts or required documents until the last day.
  • Relying on old-cycle deadlines, course lists or funding rates.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Commonwealth Shared Scholarship open to Indian students?

Yes. India is listed by the CSC as an eligible country, subject to the individual eligibility conditions and the current approved-course list.

Can I submit only the CSC application?

Indian applicants should follow the Ministry of Education notice, which requires the CSC Central and SAKSHAT portal submissions. Completing one does not replace the other.

Which deadline comes first?

The Indian SAKSHAT portal closes on 30 November 2026. CSC Central closes at 16:00 GMT on 8 December 2026.

Does the scholarship cover the full cost of study?

It covers approved tuition and specified allowances, including living support and airfare. Review the current handbook for exact entitlements and any costs or family expenses that remain your responsibility.

Can I apply for undergraduate or PhD study?

No. The Shared Scholarship is for approved full-time master's courses, not undergraduate or doctoral study.
