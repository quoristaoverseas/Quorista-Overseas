Schengen Entry/Exit System (EES) in 2026: What Indian Travellers Need to Know

A clear guide to Europe's digital border registration system, biometric collection, the 90/180-day short-stay rule and why Indian passport holders still need a Schengen visa.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 11 min readUpdated: 25 November 2026

Travellers visiting the Schengen Area may notice a different border process as Europe's Entry/Exit System (EES) replaces manual passport stamping for covered short stays. The European Commission confirms that EES became fully operational at the external borders of the participating countries on 10 April 2026.

For Indian passport holders: EES does not remove the Schengen visa requirement. Indian citizens travelling for a short visit generally still need the appropriate Schengen visa. EES is a border-registration system used when eligible travellers cross the external border; it is not a visa and is not an online permission that you apply for before travelling.

1. What is the Schengen Entry/Exit System?

EES is the European Union's digital system for recording the entry and exit of non-EU nationals travelling for short stays to the 29 European countries using the system. It records relevant travel-document details and the dates and places of entry and exit. It also captures biometric information, including a facial image and fingerprints, where the rules require it.

The system is designed to help authorities register short-stay movements more consistently and identify overstays. The progressive rollout began in October 2025 and was completed on 10 April 2026. It applies to covered non-EU travellers whether they are visa-required or visa-exempt, subject to the system's exemptions and scope.

EES is used at the participating countries' external borders. It does not mean that every internal journey between Schengen countries involves a new EES registration. The Schengen Area's internal-border arrangements are different from checks at its external borders.

2. What should Indian travellers expect?

An Indian citizen visiting for tourism, family, business or another short-stay purpose will ordinarily need a valid Schengen visa before travel. At the external Schengen border, the traveller may be registered in EES. The process can include:

  • Reading and recording passport or travel-document details.
  • Capturing a facial image.
  • Collecting fingerprints, where required and subject to applicable age and other exemptions.
  • Recording the date and place of entry or exit.
  • Checking the traveler's identity and permitted short-stay period against the relevant records.

First-time registration may take longer than a familiar passport-stamp check. Allow a sensible buffer for airport procedures, especially when you have a connecting flight, but do not assume a fixed additional processing time: queues and airport operations vary.

Children are subject to specific biometric rules and exemptions. Do not assume that every traveller, regardless of age, will provide fingerprints. Follow the instructions of the border officer and check the official EES guidance for the current rules.

3. EES and ETIAS are different — do not confuse them

The two systems are often discussed together, but they serve different functions.

Who does it concern?Most non-EU short-stay travellers, including visa-required visitorsTravellers from visa-exempt countries covered by the scheme
Do you apply before travel?No separate EES application; registration occurs at the borderYes, once the system is operational and the traveller is in scope

As checked on 25 November 2026, the official ETIAS website states that ETIAS is not yet in operation and is not collecting applications. The European Commission's traveller guidance specifically explains that an Indian citizen visiting the Schengen Area needs a visa and therefore does not need ETIAS. Once ETIAS launches, it will apply to eligible visa-exempt nationalities, not to Indian citizens travelling on an ordinary Indian passport who require a Schengen visa.

Be careful with websites claiming that Indian tourists must currently pay for an ETIAS. The official EU portal says no applications are being collected at this time. Use only the official EU website for future launch information.

4. EES does not change the 90 days in any 180-day period rule

The short-stay framework generally permits a stay of up to 90 days in any rolling 180-day period across the Schengen Area, subject to the visa or visa-exemption conditions that apply to the traveller. EES helps authorities record entries and exits; it does not grant extra days or extend a visa.

For Indian travellers, check the validity dates and number of entries on the Schengen visa sticker, and calculate the permitted stay carefully. A visa may be valid for a longer date range while still limiting the number of days that can be spent in the area. A multiple-entry visa does not mean unlimited stay.

Use the official EU short-stay calculator where appropriate and retain travel evidence such as boarding passes and itinerary records. If a journey involves several Schengen countries, remember that the short-stay calculation is for the Schengen Area as a whole, not 90 days in each country.

5. How to prepare for Schengen border checks

  • Carry the passport containing your valid visa and ensure the document is in good condition.
  • Keep evidence of accommodation, return or onward travel, trip purpose and sufficient funds accessible.
  • Be able to explain your itinerary and the reason for your visit consistently with the visa application.
  • Allow time for border processing and possible biometric registration, especially if connecting onward.
  • Check the visa's validity dates, permitted duration and number of entries before departure.
  • Do not overstay or assume that a delayed exit flight automatically extends permission.
  • Use official EU sources for EES notices and avoid unofficial sites selling an EES registration service.

Frequently asked questions

Do Indian citizens need to register for EES before travelling?

No separate pre-travel EES application is required. Registration takes place at the external border when the system applies to the traveller.

Do Indians need ETIAS for a Schengen tourist trip?

No. Indian ordinary passport holders are visa-required for short Schengen visits, so they need the appropriate Schengen visa rather than ETIAS.

Is ETIAS already accepting applications?

As checked on 25 November 2026, the official ETIAS portal says it is not yet operational and is not collecting applications. Check the official portal for the launch announcement.

Does EES replace the Schengen visa?

No. EES records covered border crossings. Travellers who need a visa must still obtain the appropriate visa before travel.

Will EES collect fingerprints from children?

Biometric collection has age-related rules and exemptions. Follow border officials' instructions and consult the official EES FAQ for the relevant age thresholds.

Does EES apply when travelling between two Schengen countries?

EES concerns crossings of the external borders of countries using the system. Ordinary travel across internal Schengen borders is a different matter, although temporary checks can occur under separate rules.
