Scholarships in Europe for Indian Students: Fully Funded and Major Options

Explore realistic European scholarship routes for Indian students, including Erasmus Mundus, DAAD EPOS, Stipendium Hungaricum and other country schemes—what they cover, eligibility limits and application routes.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 13 min readUpdated: 25 November 2026

Europe has several competitive scholarship routes for international students, but there is no single scholarship that funds every student, subject or degree. Some programmes provide a comprehensive package covering tuition, travel and living support; others offer a stipend or tuition reduction while leaving a funding gap. Indian applicants should start with the scholarship's official call, not a social-media list of “100% free universities”.

Key distinction: A scholarship can be fully funded for the specific costs named in its award rules without covering every expense a student may face. Check tuition, monthly living allowance, travel, insurance, visa costs, accommodation, deposits and dependants separately.

1. The most realistic scholarship routes to investigate

Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters — a major international route

Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters are delivered by international consortia of universities. Students may study in more than one country and graduate with a joint or multiple degree. The European Commission confirms that the best-ranked applicants worldwide may receive full scholarships covering participation costs and contributing to travel, visa and living expenses. Applicants need a bachelor's degree or must be in the final year and graduate before the programme begins.

How to apply: select a specific programme from the official Erasmus Mundus catalogue, then apply directly to that consortium's admissions process. Each programme sets its own academic prerequisites, language evidence, documents and deadline. Most applications are submitted between October and January for the following academic year, but the individual programme page controls.

Official Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters information.

DAAD Development-Related Postgraduate Courses (EPOS) — especially relevant to experienced graduates

DAAD EPOS funds selected postgraduate courses in Germany for graduates from eligible developing and newly industrialised countries. The published programme description requires at least two years of relevant professional experience. It also expects strong academic performance and a clear development-related motivation. India is a country to check in the programme's current eligible-country selector; applicants must verify that their nationality and chosen course are eligible for the specific call.

DAAD's published benefits include a monthly payment (the current programme page lists €992 for graduates), insurance contributions and a travel allowance, with possible additional support in qualifying cases. Funding normally lasts 12–42 months depending on the course. EPOS is not a general scholarship for every German master's degree: applicants must choose a course on the official EPOS list and follow that course's deadline.

DAAD India scholarship database · DAAD scholarship overview.

Stipendium Hungaricum — tuition-free study with a living-cost contribution

Hungary's Stipendium Hungaricum scheme offers tuition-free study, a monthly stipend, accommodation support and medical insurance for eligible programmes and sending-partner countries. The official programme warns that the monthly stipend and housing contribution are contributions to living costs, not a guarantee that all expenses in the chosen city will be covered. Budapest rent and other costs may exceed the housing contribution.

Indian applicants must verify that India is included as an active sending partner for the relevant call and that the chosen study field and degree level are available under the bilateral arrangement. Applications normally involve both the online scholarship system and the national sending partner's nomination/selection process. Do not assume that an application to the online portal alone completes the process.

2. Other European awards worth checking—but not automatically fully funded

France — Eiffel Excellence Scholarship

The Eiffel programme is a competitive award for international students nominated by eligible French higher-education institutions. Applicants do not submit an independent direct application to Campus France; the French institution prepares and submits the nomination. It can provide a monthly allowance and certain services, but it does not pay tuition fees. Therefore, it should not be described as a full-ride scholarship unless the host institution separately confirms a tuition waiver and the student's remaining costs are covered.

Campus France — Eiffel Excellence Scholarship.

Italy — MAECI scholarships

Italy's Ministry of Foreign Affairs offers annual scholarships for eligible foreign citizens to study or undertake research in Italy. The call specifies eligible countries, degree/research categories, duration, stipend and health coverage. Tuition treatment can depend on the university and the call; regional taxes and other costs may remain. Indian applicants should check the current annual call and its country list rather than relying on last year's deadline or award amount.

4. Who is likely to be a realistic candidate?

  • Final-year bachelor's students: Erasmus Mundus may accept applicants who are in the final year, provided they graduate before the programme begins.
  • Graduates with professional experience: DAAD EPOS is more relevant where the applicant has at least two years of professional experience and a development-related academic/professional profile.
  • Applicants open to Hungary: Stipendium Hungaricum may be relevant if the current Indian sending-partner arrangement, subject area and degree level match.
  • Applicants targeting France: Eiffel is a university-nominated award, not an application that a student independently submits. Confirm tuition funding separately.
  • Research and doctoral applicants: check country-specific research grants and university-funded doctoral positions; many are employment or research contracts rather than taught-degree scholarships.

5. Documents to prepare before the call opens

  • Passport and nationality evidence
  • Degree certificates and transcripts
  • Class 10/12 records if requested
  • CV tailored to the scholarship
  • Motivation letter or study plan
  • Academic/professional references
  • Language test or accepted exemption proof
  • Work-experience certificates where required
  • Research proposal for research awards
  • Portfolio or writing sample where relevant
  • Certified translations if required
  • Programme-specific application forms

6. A practical application strategy

  1. Choose the degree level, subject area and countries you can genuinely study in.
  2. Search the official scholarship database or programme catalogue; shortlist the specific eligible courses.
  3. Read the scholarship call and the university admission page separately. Scholarship eligibility does not automatically mean admission eligibility.
  4. Check whether the scheme requires direct application, university nomination, a national sending-partner nomination or more than one application.
  5. Calculate the funding gap, including flights, visa fees, initial accommodation, deposits, uncovered tuition and any months outside the scholarship period.
  6. Prepare references and language evidence early. Do not wait until the final week to request university documents.
  7. Submit before the official deadline and save the exact call, application receipt and submitted documents.

7. Common mistakes and funding gaps

  • Calling a stipend plus housing contribution “all expenses paid” when the scheme says it is only a contribution.
  • Assuming every Indian applicant can apply to a bilateral scholarship without checking the current sending-partner agreement.
  • Using last year's deadlines, benefits or country lists for a new call.
  • Confusing scholarship application with university admission or national nomination.
  • Ignoring tuition charges, deposits, travel, visa fees, insurance exclusions and costs before the first stipend arrives.
  • Paying an agent or intermediary who promises guaranteed selection. Scholarships are awarded by the administering institution or government, not guaranteed by a consultant.

8. When should Indian students prepare?

For many Erasmus Mundus programmes, applications for the next academic year are submitted between October and January. DAAD EPOS deadlines vary by course and can fall many months before the intended start. Other national schemes publish annual calls with their own schedules. Build a calendar from the official page for each programme, and treat any third-party calendar as a reminder—not the authoritative deadline.

Frequently asked questions

Can Indian students apply for Erasmus Mundus scholarships?

Yes. Erasmus Mundus scholarships are open to the best-ranked applicants worldwide, subject to each joint master's programme's admission criteria and scholarship rules.

Does Erasmus Mundus cover all expenses?

The European Commission says scholarships cover participation costs and contribute to travel, visa and living expenses. Check the individual programme's scholarship agreement and plan for any expenses not expressly covered.

Can a fresh graduate apply for DAAD EPOS?

Usually not under the published standard criteria: EPOS requires at least two years of professional experience. Check the current call for any programme-specific conditions.

Does Stipendium Hungaricum fully cover living expenses?

No. Its official website says the stipend and accommodation contribution are contributions and may not cover all living costs, particularly in larger cities.

Does the Eiffel Scholarship pay French university tuition?

No. Campus France states that tuition fees are not covered by the Eiffel programme. Confirm whether the host institution offers a separate exemption.

Are these scholarships open right now?

Calls and deadlines change each year. Check the official scheme and university pages for the current intake; do not rely on an old deadline or a previous year's eligibility list.
