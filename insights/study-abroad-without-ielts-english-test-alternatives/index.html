Study Abroad Without IELTS: English Test Alternatives

Explore IELTS alternatives for Indian students applying abroad, including TOEFL, PTE, Duolingo, Cambridge tests and university-specific waivers.

✦ Quorista Overseas Editorial Team◷ 14 min readPublished: 25 November 2026

IELTS is a widely recognised English-language test, but it is not the only way to demonstrate English proficiency for an overseas degree. Depending on the university, programme, intake and applicant's previous education, an institution may accept another test, an English-medium education waiver, an interview or its own assessment.

The phrase “study abroad without IELTS” needs careful interpretation. It may mean that IELTS is not the particular test required. It does not automatically mean that no English-language evidence is needed. A student may still need TOEFL, PTE Academic, Duolingo English Test, a Cambridge qualification or a formal waiver.

Before making a decision: obtain confirmation for the exact programme and intake. A waiver granted by an admissions office does not automatically apply to a scholarship, professional regulator, immigration authority or another university.

Is IELTS mandatory for studying abroad?

No single IELTS rule applies to every international university or destination. Each institution publishes its own accepted tests, minimum scores, validity periods and waiver conditions. Requirements can also differ between undergraduate and postgraduate programmes, faculties, campuses and intakes.

There are three separate questions to answer:

  • Academic admission: what evidence does the university or department require before it will issue an offer?
  • Scholarship or sponsor eligibility: does the funding scheme impose a separate test, score or language condition?
  • Immigration or visa: does the relevant authority require language evidence or a particular approved test for this visa route?

Do not treat a university's general country page as the final authority if the department publishes a more specific rule. Read the current programme page and the admissions guide for the intended intake.

These tests assess English ability, but they are not interchangeable in every admissions system. Check whether the institution accepts the exact test version, delivery format and score you plan to submit.

Can a Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate replace IELTS?

Sometimes, but MOI is a waiver route, not a universal substitute. A Medium of Instruction certificate generally confirms that a previous qualification was taught, and sometimes assessed, in English. Universities may accept it for selected applicants if their policy permits.

Possible conditions include:

  • The previous qualification was a full degree, not merely a short course or a few English-taught subjects.
  • The degree was taught and assessed in English throughout, or for a minimum period specified by the university.
  • The awarding institution is recognised and the qualification meets the university's academic entry rules.
  • The certificate is issued by the university's Registrar, Controller of Examinations or another authorised office.
  • The certificate identifies the applicant, programme, study dates, mode of instruction and language of assessment.
  • The waiver is available to applicants from the country and institution concerned, and is still in force for the selected intake.

A university may reject an MOI letter if it is self-prepared, signed by an unauthorised person, vague about the language of assessment, or inconsistent with transcripts. Some universities accept MOI only for particular countries or institutions; others require a test regardless of the medium of previous study.

MOI document checklist

  • Official letter on institutional letterhead
  • Full legal name and student identification details
  • Exact qualification and subject
  • Study start and completion dates
  • Confirmation of English as teaching language
  • Confirmation of English as assessment/examination language, if true
  • Authorised signature, designation and contact details
  • Stamp, verification or sealed issue where required
  • Transcript and degree/provisional certificate
  • Written university approval of the waiver

Never ask a college to state something inaccurate. If only some modules were taught in English, the letter should not claim the entire degree was English-medium.

Country, scholarship and visa differences

It is risky to say that an entire country accepts or rejects IELTS waivers. Requirements are normally set at a more specific level. For example, one university may accept a particular English test while another does not; one department may allow MOI while another requires a test.

For the UK, check the university's admissions policy and whether the programme or immigration route has additional English-language rules. For the United States, review the institution's international graduate admissions page and the individual department's rules. Across Europe, requirements vary by country, institution and programme; do not assume that an English-taught course automatically waives proof of proficiency. The same principle applies in Asia and other destinations.

Scholarships can have their own conditions. A university waiver should not be assumed to satisfy a government scholarship, external sponsor or professional licensing body. If the course leads to a regulated profession, check the regulator's language rules as well.

How to verify a waiver before applying

  1. Identify the exact course. Record the university, campus, degree title, department and intake.
  2. Open the current admissions guide. Check the accepted tests, minimum overall scores, section scores and validity dates.
  3. Read the waiver wording. Look for country-specific, institution-specific or qualification-specific conditions.
  4. Check the MOI format. Confirm who must issue it and what it must say.
  5. Separate other rules. Review scholarship, visa and professional accreditation requirements independently.
  6. Ask the department in writing. Attach a transcript or sample MOI only through the official admissions channel and request confirmation for the intake.
  7. Keep the reply. Save the dated email and the policy page used when deciding not to take a test.

Practical risk check: If the deadline is close and the university has not confirmed a waiver in writing, do not build the entire application plan around an assumption. Check whether there is enough time to book an accepted test as a backup.

Real alternatives: published English-language routes

These examples show the difference between an accepted alternative test and a genuine exemption. They are published institutional rules, not blanket promises for every course or applicant.

University of East London: several tests besides IELTS

The University of East London lists TOEFL iBT, Pearson PTE Academic, Trinity ISE and Cambridge IGCSE among accepted English qualifications for relevant applicants. Its India guidance also describes a limited undergraduate route for students educated in English throughout their studies who meet specified results in named Indian Class 12 certificates. The undergraduate exception should not be assumed to apply to postgraduate applicants; postgraduate applicants must meet the applicable course-level English requirement.

Check UEL's India-specific guidance and course requirements.

Sapienza University of Rome: PTE and TOEFL are listed, but India's MOI is not a blanket exemption

Sapienza's 2026 English-language requirements list IELTS, TOEFL iBT, Cambridge qualifications and PTE Academic as accepted evidence for the relevant B2 requirement. Its published exemption for an English-taught diploma is restricted to specified countries; India is not on that list. This is a useful example of why an MOI certificate should never be assumed to create an exemption.

Read Sapienza's 2026 English-language requirements.

How to use these examples: First check the exact course and intake. Then confirm the required score, validity period, test version, minimum sub-scores and whether the rule applies to your previous qualification. An accepted alternative test is still an English-language test; an MOI exemption is a separate decision.

Editorial note: This guide is general information, not a promise of a waiver or visa outcome. University policies and immigration rules change. Verify the current programme and intake requirements directly with the institution and relevant authority before paying fees or making travel plans.
